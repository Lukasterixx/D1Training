# Novelty check and publication venues: whole-body force amplification for a weak arm on a legged base (Go2 + D1)

Research date: 2026-10-01. Searches: web search over arXiv (cs.RO), conference sites, IEEE-RAS pages; abstracts and HTML full texts fetched where possible. "Fetched" means the page was read; "snippet" means only a search-result summary was seen.

## Q1. Novelty: does any 2025-2026 work already do "force-capability analysis + whole-body policy that picks posture/contact so a weak arm on a legged base exceeds its joint-torque rating on handles/doors"?

### Takeaway
Nothing found does the full combination. The pieces exist separately. (a) Learned force-adaptive or force-commandable whole-body policies on quadrupeds and humanoids (Portela 2024, UniFP 2025, FALCON 2025/26, HAFO, FAME, a kinematics-aware G1 paper, FACET). (b) Force-capability (residual force polytope) optimisation for mobile manipulators, model-based and not legged (FORTE, Sept 2026). (c) Learned door and articulated-object opening with legged manipulators, without force-capability reasoning (Zhang et al. 2024, OpenHEART 2026, sensorless force-aware wheeled-legged papers from Sept 2026). The closest "scoop" risk is the observation itself. Portela et al. (2024) already measured a 90 N whole-body pull from a Z1 arm rated at 36 N payload and named "optimizing the whole-body posture for applying large forces" as a use case. The student's paper therefore has to claim the analysis, the choice of force paths that bypass the arm's motors, the weak servo arm and the lever/door task, not the bare idea that the body adds force. Two September 2026 preprints (FORTE, 2609.21497; force-aware wheeled-legged RL, 2609.13779) show the space is moving fast, so the novelty check should be repeated before submission.

### Cited Findings

**Closest prior: body posture amplifying arm force (quadruped + arm)**
- Portela, Margolis, Ji, Agrawal (MIT), "Learning Force Control for Legged Manipulation" (arXiv 2405.01402, May 2024). An RL policy on a Unitree B1 + Z1 arm takes commanded end-effector forces (−70 to 70 N per axis). They measured a **maximum pulling force of 90 N, exceeding the arm's rated 36 N payload**, and list "optimizing the whole-body posture for applying large forces across a larger workspace" as a motivating use. Hardware forces were measured with a **dynamometer at five gripper positions**. The whole-body workspace is 1.29 m³ against 0.81 m³ for the fixed arm (+59%). There are no ablations or quantitative baselines; door opening is shown only qualitatively. — [arXiv 2405.01402 (HTML, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01402v1)
  - Gap left: the paper does not analyse *why* the body adds force (which joints saturate, whether the reaction path bypasses the arm motors). It does not optimise or reward force capability; the posture benefit emerges. Its arm (Z1) is much stronger than a servo arm like the D1, and it has no lever/spring-loaded task with measured success. (Venue: I believe ICRA 2024, but my fetch showed only the arXiv listing; unconfirmed.)
- Zhi, Li, Yin, Jia, Huang, "Learning a Unified Policy for Position and Force Control in Legged Loco-Manipulation" (UniFP, arXiv 2505.20829). One RL policy for position tracking, force application, force tracking and compliance, with **force estimated from proprioceptive history and no F/T sensor**. Platforms: Unitree B2-Z1 and G1. — [arXiv 2505.20829 (abstract, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20829); a search-result snippet of a later version says it "demonstrates whole-body large force pulling by specifying high impedance values ... significantly wider pulling range than baselines" — [arXiv 2505.20829v1 snippet](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20829v1). Search summaries say it was presented at CoRL 2025 (not confirmed on the CoRL site).
  - Gap left: force is a command to track, not a capability to maximise. There is no posture/contact selection for force and no weak-arm torque-saturation analysis.

**Humanoid force-adaptive loco-manipulation (FALCON and follow-ups)**
- FALCON (Zhang, Yuan, ..., He, Shi; LeCAR Lab/CMU), arXiv 2505.06776, **L4DC 2026 (oral)**, PMLR v331. Dual-agent RL (separate lower- and upper-body agents) with a **3D force curriculum that enforces joint-torque feasibility via inverse dynamics**. It explicitly addresses weak joints such as the G1 wrists. Reports 2x more accurate upper-body joint tracking under force disturbances. Validated on Unitree G1 and Booster T1. — [PMLR v331](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v331/zhang26a.html); [GitHub (L4DC 2026 Oral)](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/FALCON); [arXiv 2505.06776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06776)
  - Gap left: it *tolerates* external forces within torque limits. It does not choose postures or contacts to *raise* the force the end effector can deliver, and it targets humanoids, not a quadruped with a separate weak arm.
- HAFO (arXiv 2511.20275; a ScienceDirect journal version also exists). Dual-agent RL with spring-damper-modelled pulling disturbances and a progressive curriculum. The lower body "knows" when the upper body will take load so it can brace or shift the CoM. Shown under heavy load, thrust and rope suspension. — [arXiv 2511.20275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20275); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0967066126002224)
- FAME (Pudasaini, Zhang, Lavering, Roncone, Correll; CU Boulder), arXiv 2603.08961, March 2026. A standing policy for the Unitree H12 conditioned on a latent of upper-body configuration and hand forces. The hand force is estimated sensor-free as F = −(Jᵀ)†(τ − τ_g). Sim force range Fx,Fy ∈ [−20,20] N, Fz ∈ [−30,30] N. Sim success: FAME 73.84%, Base+Curriculum 51.40%, Base 29.44%. Hardware results are qualitative with no trial counts. — [arXiv 2603.08961 (HTML, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08961v1)
- Xiao, Xu, Zhe, Liu, Chen, "Kinematics-Aware Multi-Policy RL for Force-Capable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation" (arXiv 2511.21169, Nov 2025). Unitree G1. Three-stage decoupled policies (upper, lower, delta-command) with a force curriculum (max force goal 50 N). Demonstrations: carrying 4 kg; pushing/pulling a cart with a 112.8 kg total load, with applied forces up to 40 N push and 35 N pull. Ablations are internal only; no external baselines, no trial counts, and the hardware force measurement method is not stated. — [arXiv 2511.21169 (HTML, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21169)
  - Gap left: none of these treats a weak arm's joint torque as the binding constraint to be bypassed by posture or contact.
- FACET (arXiv 2505.06883): RL policies imitate a virtual mass-spring-damper (impedance reference tracking) for legged robots under external forces. — [arXiv 2505.06883](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06883)

**Force capability / force polytope optimisation (model-based)**
- FORTE (Wang, Zhang, Solak, Zhao, Ajoudani; arXiv 2609.21497, **submitted 18 Sept 2026**). Defines task-oriented force capability as "the signed distance between a task-force uncertainty ball and the dynamic residual force polytope (RFP)". A VLM infers object properties and task forces, then whole-body multi-objective trajectory optimisation runs over force capability, manipulability, joint limits, smoothness and base motion. Hardware: a (wheeled, as far as the abstract shows) mobile manipulator doing lifting and holding under varying payloads. Baselines: RFP inscribed radius, RFP cone, manipulability-only. Code is released. — [arXiv 2609.21497 (abstract, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21497)
  - Gap left: optimisation rather than an RL policy, not a legged base, no contact or bracing selection, no door/lever task. It is the closest *formal* precedent for the "force-capability analysis" half, so it must be cited and contrasted.
- The residual force polytope itself is older: it represents the task-space forces a robot can still resist given whole-body dynamics and the torques left after a nominal trajectory. — [Residual Force Polytope, arXiv 1908.05380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.05380); [Robotics & Autonomous Systems version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921889021000993)
- "Enabling extended tool manipulation through redundancy-driven contact exploitation" (Scientific Reports, 2026). Snippet only; the full text was paywalled to my fetcher. It states that forces at a distance on extended tools "amplify joint torques through the lever-arm effect" for torque-limited manipulators, and it induces environmental support contact through redundancy resolution so the task stays dynamically feasible. — [Scientific Reports (snippet)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-60582-y)
  - Relevance: the same "bracing/support contact bypasses weak joints" idea, on a (seemingly) fixed-base arm with a model-based method. Platform and numbers were not verified.

**Door / articulated-object opening with legged manipulators**
- Zhang, Ma, Miki, Hutter (ETH), "Learning to Open and Traverse Doors with a Legged Manipulator" (arXiv 2409.04882, CoRL 2024). ANYmal + arm with a hook end-effector; teacher-student RL. Door hinge/handle resistance is randomised 0–30 N·m, with door closers, 15–75 kg door mass and 15–90° handle angles. Hardware: **pull side 20/20, push side 18/20 (95%)**. The applied wrench was not estimated; it was set to a fixed mean value. Arm torque limits were enforced by clipping the PD targets. — [arXiv 2409.04882 (HTML, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04882v1)
- OpenHEART (Lim, Lee, Lee, Nahrendra, Myung; arXiv 2603.05830, March 2026; the fetched HTML reportedly says ICRA 2026, unconfirmed). **Unitree Go2 + ViperX 300 arm**. Covers 41 PartManip articulated objects: revolute and prismatic joints; bar, handle and knob handles. Sim success is 79.35% train and 79.02% test. Handle resistance is *not* modelled, and the manipulator friction is set to zero with a hook added to the gripper. Real-world demos are qualitative with no trial counts. — [arXiv 2603.05830 (HTML, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05830)
- Zeng et al., "Force-Aware RL with Hybrid Sensorless Force Estimation for Wheeled-Legged Loco-Manipulation" (arXiv 2609.13779, 12 Sept 2026). The end-effector force estimate (generalized-momentum observer + contact-constrained wrench projection + learned residual) is an explicit policy observation. Demos: valve rotation, wiping, force-guided door opening, zero-force guidance. — [arXiv 2609.13779 (abstract, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13779). A search snippet (same query) also describes a spring-loaded door with a restoring torque and a DeepRobotics M20S + 6-DoF arm tested with and without a door closer giving **50 N at the handle**. I could not confirm from the abstract which of the Sept 2026 papers these snippet details belong to. — [search snippet source list incl. 2609.13779 / 2609.03889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779)
- FWBC-VLA (arXiv 2609.03889, 3 Sept 2026). Wheeled-legged + arm. A sensorless force estimator detects contact onset and sustained load, and whole-body compensation actions use the body and legs to stabilise against manipulation forces. Demos: whiteboard wiping and **door opening with a door closer**; more than 5,000 training episodes. — [arXiv 2609.03889 (abstract, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03889)
  - Gap left (all four): the force is estimated and compensated or tracked, but none analyses or maximises how much force a weak arm can deliver through posture or contact choice.
- DoorBot (ICRA 2025): haptic, current-threshold feedback for unlock-lever and unlock-knob primitives on cabinets. — [DoorBot project page](https://tx-leo.github.io/DoorBot/)

**Same hardware (Go2 + D1)**
- Schperberg, Wang, Di Cairano (MERL), "Safe Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation via Combined Model and Learning-based Control" (arXiv 2603.02443, **ICRA 2026, Vienna**). **Unitree Go2 + D1 6-DoF servo arm + wrist BOTA 6-DoF F/T sensor**. Model-based admittance control on the arm (external wrench → EE velocity) with an RL locomotion policy, a Reference Governor for safety and an NN-augmented Kalman filter. Their search snippet notes "**D1 arm motor send/receive frequency is limited at 40 Hz**", so arm admittance runs at 40 Hz and locomotion RL at 50 Hz. — [arXiv 2603.02443 (abstract, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02443); [PDF snippet for 40 Hz](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02443)
  - Gap left: compliance/safety in human interaction; no force amplification, posture-for-force or door/lever task.
- Caltech SURF 2025 report: integrating the D1 arm with the Obelisk interface for Go2 loco-manipulation (a student report, not a paper). — [Zeng, SURF report](https://eloisezeng.people.caltech.edu/documents/32338/2025_SURF_Final_Report.pdf)
- No 2025–2026 paper was found that uses a D1 (or any low-cost servo arm) on a quadruped for a high-force task. Other Go2-arm papers use different arms: TAC-LOCO uses a Go2 + WidowX 250, FT-WBC a Go2 + Airbot Play. — [TAC-LOCO](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10132v1); [FT-WBC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24466)

**Older prior on body/leaning for large forces (outside 2025–26, but reviewers will expect it)**
- Whole-body bilateral teleoperation of a wheeled humanoid: the tasks needed whole-body coordination to apply large forces, and the robot "could not succeed by leaning with their body or pushing with their arms alone" (2023). — [arXiv 2307.01350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01350)
- Optimisation of body-ground contact to augment quadruped whole-body loco-manipulation (2020). — [arXiv 2002.10552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.10552)
- Pedipulate (manipulation with a quadruped leg, 2024). — [arXiv 2402.10837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10837)

### Inferences
- **The novel claim is not "body adds force".** Portela et al. already show 90 N against a 36 N rating, and UniFP shows whole-body high-force pulling. A defensible contribution would combine four things. (1) A force-path analysis that explains *why* naive crouching gives only about 1.3x (the reaction still loads the shoulder and elbow) and which postures or contacts bypass the arm's motors (near-singular or locked-out arm, bracing, tool moment arm, leg/body contact). (2) A policy that is *rewarded or conditioned on force capability* (for example an RFP-style margin of the weak arm) rather than only tracking a force command. (3) A very weak servo arm (D1) where the torque limit truly binds. (4) Spring-loaded lever/door tasks with measured forces. None of the papers above does (1)+(2)+(3)+(4) together.
- **Most direct competitors to position against:** Portela 2024 (the empirical amplification precedent), UniFP 2025 (unified force/position policy on quadrupeds), FALCON 2026 (torque-feasible force curricula), FORTE 2026 (force-capability optimisation), and Zhang 2024 / OpenHEART 2026 / the Sept 2026 wheeled-legged force-aware door papers (task precedents).
- The D1's 40 Hz command limit (reported by Schperberg et al.) and its servo-based torque limits help the "weak arm" story. They also constrain how dynamic any force-exploiting behaviour can be on hardware.
- Three relevant preprints appeared in September 2026 alone (2609.03889, 2609.13779, 2609.21497), so scoop risk is real and growing. Re-run this search (arXiv cs.RO, keywords "force capability", "force polytope", "leverage", "weak arm", "torque-limited", "door", "lever") right before submitting.

### Gaps
- Could not access full text of the Scientific Reports extended-tool paper; platform, numbers and whether it is legged are unverified.
- Could not confirm Portela et al.'s and UniFP's final venues from official proceedings (ICRA 2024 and CoRL 2025 respectively are my best understanding/search summaries).
- The 50 N door-closer and spring-loaded-door details came from a search snippet and could not be tied with certainty to 2609.13779 versus 2609.03889.
- Google Scholar was not directly queryable; coverage relies on web search over arXiv and publisher pages. Non-English (e.g., Chinese-journal) work on "body weight" force exertion with quadrupeds may be missed.
- I did not find any paper using the words "force amplification" or "weak arm" for legged manipulation. That absence is weak evidence; the idea may appear under other wording ("manipulation envelope", "force-capable", "high-payload").

## Q3. Evaluation expectations: what accepted papers in this area include, and what a credible "force amplification" claim needs

### Takeaway
Accepted papers in this niche typically combine four things: hardware success rates over 20–50 trials per condition, a position-only or no-force baseline, internal ablations, and (rarely) an external force measurement such as a dynamometer, F/T sensor or load cell. Hardware force evidence is often thin; several high-profile papers show forces only qualitatively. That leaves room for a paper whose core evidence is *measured* force and torque margins. Reviewers will likely require exactly that for an "amplification" claim, compared at least against arm-only and naive body motion, on more than one task.

### Cited Findings
- **Portela et al. 2024 (B1 + Z1):** hardware force tracking measured with a **dynamometer at 5 gripper positions**; maximum pull 90 N against a 36 N rated payload; workspace volume comparison (1.29 against 0.81 m³). **No ablations or quantitative baselines**; tasks are qualitative demos. — [arXiv 2405.01402](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01402v1)
- **UniFP 2025 (B2-Z1 and G1):** force commands of 0–60 N with **average error within 10 N (5–10 N at five EE positions)**; a 2.5 kg dumbbell balanced with a 25 N upward command. Four contact-rich imitation tasks, **each evaluated 50 times**; demonstrations of 30 episodes per task (5,050 for wiping). Baseline: vision-only position-control policy. Success: wipe 0.22→0.58, open cabinet 0.36→0.70, close cabinet 0.30→0.72, open drawer (occluded) 0.30→0.76 (~39.5% higher). — [arXiv 2505.20829 (HTML v1, fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20829v1)
- **Zhang et al. 2024 (ANYmal door traversal, CoRL):** 20/20 pull-side and 18/20 push-side hardware traversals (95%), with failures attributed to unmodelled doorway geometry. Ablations: removing the recurrence makes the policy fail, removing the door-type loss has little effect, and a teacher without privileged info does worse. Zero-shot sim-to-real via domain randomisation of PD gains, door dynamics and noise. — [arXiv 2409.04882](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04882v1)
- **FALCON (L4DC 2026 oral):** two humanoid platforms (G1, T1), a quantitative tracking-error comparison (2x better upper-body tracking), and a torque-feasible force curriculum. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v331/zhang26a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06776)
- **OpenHEART (Go2 + ViperX; reportedly ICRA 2026):** 41 sim objects with train/test success (79.35/79.02%), comparison against a center-based teacher and a point-cloud policy, and four ablations. Real-world results are qualitative only. — [arXiv 2603.05830](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05830)
- **Schperberg et al. (Go2 + D1 + BOTA F/T, ICRA 2026):** validated "in both simulation and hardware"; trial counts are not in the abstract. Notable for adding an **external wrist F/T sensor** to the D1. — [arXiv 2603.02443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02443)
- **FORTE (preprint, Sept 2026):** baselines are alternative capability metrics (RFP inscribed radius, RFP cone, manipulability-only) on hardware lifting/holding tasks under varied payloads. — [arXiv 2609.21497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21497)
- **Preprints with weak hardware evidence:** FAME has qualitative hardware and sim-only success statistics ([arXiv 2603.08961](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08961v1)). The kinematics-aware G1 paper has no trial counts and does not state its force measurement method ([arXiv 2511.21169](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21169)).
- **Venue norms:** CoRL requires a Limitations section and encourages real-robot results ([CoRL CFP](https://www.corl.org/contributions/call-for-papers)). RSS Stage 2 evaluations are set by an Extension Charter from Stage 1 review ([RSS CFP](https://roboticsconference.org/information/cfp/)).

### Inferences (what reviewers would likely require for a credible "force amplification" claim)
These are my synthesis from the patterns above, not quoted reviewer guidance.
1. **Measured force at the handle on hardware**, from a load cell or F/T sensor in the lever/door mechanism or a dynamometer as in Portela. Policy-internal force *estimates* (UniFP, FAME, the Sept 2026 sensorless papers) will not be accepted as proof of amplification. Report maximum sustained force and force at task success.
2. **Measured arm joint torque or current margins** (D1 servo load/current readings) alongside the handle force. This shows the reaction path really bypasses the shoulder and elbow motors, which is the paper's mechanistic claim. A ratio such as handle-force per peak arm-joint torque utilisation gives a clean metric.
3. **Baselines:** (a) arm-only with a fixed base, (b) naive body motion (crouch/lean with the arm rigid; the student's 1.3x case), (c) a position-only whole-body policy, (d) a force-aware policy without the capability term or posture/contact freedom, and ideally (e) a model-based posture optimiser (FORTE/RFP style) to separate "learning" from "analysis".
4. **Ablations:** remove the force-capability observation or reward; disallow bracing or leg/body contact; disallow singular/locked-out postures; vary the spring stiffness and required torque.
5. **More than one task or mechanism:** at least a spring-loaded lever and a door (push and pull), ideally with a sweep of resistance levels so the success-against-required-force curve can be shown for each method. Zhang et al. randomised resistance to 30 N·m in sim; the student's combiner-box springs reach about 0.4 N·m (per the repo commit message), so a stiffer adjustable test rig will probably be needed for the amplification result to be striking.
6. **Hardware trial counts of about 10–20 per condition minimum**, with success rates and confidence intervals. Zhang et al. used 20 per side and UniFP 50 per task.
7. **Sim-to-real evidence for force:** compare sim-predicted against measured handle force and arm torque for the same postures, especially because the D1 is a servo arm with a 40 Hz command limit (Schperberg et al.).
8. **An honest scope/limitations section:** which force paths transfer, failure modes (slip, base tipping, servo overheating) and safety (CoRL requires this explicitly).
