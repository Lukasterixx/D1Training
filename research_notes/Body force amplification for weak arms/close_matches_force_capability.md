# Close matches to "a legged body amplifying a weak arm's force": full-text verification of seven papers

Method note (applies to every finding below): all seven papers were downloaded from arXiv as PDFs on 2026-10-01 and read in full as text (pdftotext). The arXiv version read is given for each paper, because three of them have been revised (Thor is on v4). "Full text" in a citation means the fact was read in the body of the paper, not taken from the abstract. Venue facts come from the arXiv comments field, the paper's own header, or Crossref metadata, as marked. Nothing below is filled in from an abstract unless it is labelled "abstract only".

## Q1. Does each paper exist, and what exactly does it do? (platform, arm, task, method, how force capability or torque limits enter, measured forces, baselines, trial counts, limitations)

### Takeaway
All seven papers exist and the claims in the brief broadly hold, with caveats that matter. Portela's 90 N is a single pull compared against a payload rating, with no measuring instrument named. RoLoMa's lever "50–69 N to 68–75 N" values are computed robustness margins, not measured lever forces. Thor never discusses arm joint torques. Sumo measures no forces at all. FORTE computes capability for the arm alone and treats the base as kinematic. The two wheeled-legged preprints work at 15–50 N and give no door or lever torque in N·m.

### Cited Findings

#### 1. Portela, Margolis, Ji, Agrawal, "Learning Force Control for Legged Manipulation" (arXiv 2405.01402, v2 read)
- Exists: arXiv 2405.01402, submitted 2 May 2024 (v2 20 May 2024). The comments field says it was accepted to ICRA 2024 and the ICRA 2024 loco-manipulation workshop. — [arXiv abs page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01402)
- Venue confirmed: 2024 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), pp. 15366–15372, DOI 10.1109/ICRA57147.2024.10611066, dated 13 May 2024. — [Crossref record via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICRA57147.2024.10611066). Thor's reference list cites the same pages. — [Thor v4 full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Platform: Unitree B1 quadruped (55 kg, 0.64 m tall, policy at 50 Hz on a Jetson Xavier NX) with a Unitree Z1 6-DoF arm (5.3 kg, 0.74 m maximum reach). — [full text, Sec. III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Control interface: one RL policy outputs 17 joint position targets for PD controllers (the 12 leg joints and the first 5 arm joints). The last two arm joints (gripper roll and opening) come straight from the joystick. — [full text, Sec. III–IV-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Method: PPO in Isaac Gym. The policy is commanded with an end-effector force (±70 N per axis, world frame), an end-effector position, a base velocity and a binary force/position mode flag. External force is applied in simulation by a spring-damper "potential field" pulling the gripper toward a randomized setpoint (Kp = 700, Kd = 6). There is no force sensor; a concurrent state estimator predicts the gripper force from proprioceptive history. — [full text, Sec. IV-B, IV-C, IV-E](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- How torque limits enter: the reward has an indicator penalty when an arm joint torque exceeds 0.8 of its maximum, with weight −0.0015. It sits inside the exponential "safety and smoothness" term, next to arm joint-limit (−3.0) and collision (−5.0) penalties. There is no force-capability metric, no constraint and no posture objective. — [full text, Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Posture claim: the introduction lists "optimizing the whole-body posture for applying large forces across a larger workspace" as one use of learned force control. No experiment optimizes posture explicitly. — [full text, Sec. I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- The 90 N result in full: "To evaluate whether our policy can coordinate the body with the legs to increase the applied force, we initialized the arm directly in front of the robot and recorded the highest pulling force we can achieve … The observed force of 90 N is greater than the arm's rated payload of 36 N." The paper does not name the instrument, the number of trials or the pull direction for this result. — [full text, Sec. V-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Quantitative hardware force test: the end effector was attached to a dynamometer and commanded downward forces from 0 to 70 N. There were five trials, with the gripper high, low, left, right and middle. Tracking error was 5–10 N, close to simulation. The policy's own force estimate overshot. — [full text, Sec. V-A, Fig. 4D–E](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Baselines: the only arm-only baseline is kinematic. A fixed-base arm workspace from 3000 sampled configurations (0.81 m³) is compared with the whole-body policy (1.29 m³), 59% larger. No arm-only or body-posture-off force baseline is reported. — [full text, Sec. V-D, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Other results: gravity compensation with 0, 2, 4 and 6 kg payloads (compliance restored up to 4 kg). Mean position error is about 4.6 / 4.8 / 5.5 cm in simulation and 4.42 / 5.37 / 6.86 cm on hardware with motion capture. — [full text, Sec. V-C, V-D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Stated limitations: around 5 cm position and 5 N force accuracy may not be enough for precise tasks, and "It is unknown whether this performance represents a limit of the hardware, optimization method, or choice of policy architecture." — [full text, Sec. VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- The project page named in the paper (tif-twirl-13.github.io/learning-compliance) returned GitHub Pages 404 on 2026-10-01, so the supplement (force tracking across the workspace) could not be checked. — [project URL as printed in the paper](https://tif-twirl-13.github.io/learning-compliance/)

#### 2. Wang, Zhang, Solak, Zhao, Ajoudani, "FORTE: Task-Adaptive Force Capability Optimization for Mobile Manipulators" (arXiv 2609.21497, v1 read)
- Exists: arXiv 2609.21497, v1 18 Sep 2026. No venue is listed; the abstract page has no comments or journal reference. Authors are from the IIT HRI² Lab (Genoa) and Xi'an Jiaotong University. — [arXiv abs page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21497); [full text header](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Platform: MOCA, a mobile collaborative robot with a velocity-controlled wheeled base and a torque-controlled 7-DoF Franka Emika arm. A basket and a RealSense D435i are mounted on the end effector. — [full text, Sec. 3.1, 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Arm torque limits used: "conservative" limits of 60 N·m for joints 1–4 and 12 N·m for joints 5–7. These are imposed "to prevent actuator saturation" and used in the residual force polytope (RFP). — [full text, Eq. 38](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Method: a VLM (GPT-4o) estimates the payload mass from an RGB image and a prompt. The desired task force at each waypoint is f = m̂(p̈ − g). The force-capability metric is the signed distance between a task-force uncertainty ball (radius r = 10 N) and the dynamic RFP, P = {f : τ̃⁻ ≤ Jᵀf ≤ τ̃⁺}. Here τ̃ are the arm torque limits minus the RNEA torque of the nominal trajectory, so the robot can still follow that trajectory while producing f. — [full text, Sec. 3.2, 4.1, 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- How the metric enters: an exponential penalty applies when the signed distance is negative. It is one of five weighted costs: force capability, manipulability, joint-limit avoidance, smoothness and base-oscillation suppression (a DCT term). These are solved in a whole-body NLP with IPOPT under end-effector pose equality constraints. Weights stay fixed across payloads. — [full text, Sec. 4.2–4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Key scope limit: "the force capability analysis presented in this work is performed only for the manipulator arm … the mobile base is regarded as a kinematic redundancy source … The residual force polytope is computed solely from the arm actuator torque limits." Wheel actuation and stability constraints are neglected. — [full text, Sec. 3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Tasks: lifting a 0.33 L or a 2.7 L beverage load in the basket along a fixed Cartesian trajectory, and single-point holding at a fixed pose. There is also a 12 s MuJoCo simulation with time-varying task forces (6 runs per method) and a VLM mass-estimation benchmark (Image2Mass household set: 56 items, 423 images). — [full text, Sec. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Measured forces: none. All "force capability" curves are model-computed directional RFP capacity along the task-force direction. — [full text, Sec. 5.3–5.4, Figs. 8–9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Hardware outcomes: there were 3 trials per method in the lifting experiment. With 2.7 L, manipulability-only optimization lost lift in 1 of 3 trials at about 8 s. Unoptimized quintic interpolation failed at about 2.5 s. With 2.7 L the teaching-point configuration "exhibits a noticeably lower arm posture", which the authors read as insufficient force capability. — [full text, Sec. 5.3–5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Baselines: manipulability-only, RFP inscribed radius and RFP cone (both from Ferrolho et al.), a teaching point, and quintic interpolation. There is no body-posture-off or arm-only force comparison; the base only repositions the arm. — [full text, Sec. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Stated future work and limitations: only translational force is modelled (extending to 6-DoF wrench is future work), there is no online replanning, and pushing and drawer opening are not tested. Code is at github.com/yeying256/FORTE (not visited) and data are on Mendeley. — [full text, Sec. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)

#### 3. Li, Shi, Shi, Hu, Ma, Wang, Wang, Luo, "Thor: Towards Human-Inspired Whole-Body Reactions for Intense Contact-Rich Environments" (arXiv 2510.26280, v4 read; v1 checked)
- Exists: arXiv 2510.26280. v1 was 30 Oct 2025 and v4 21 Sep 2026; the v1 title said "Human-Level" and v4 says "Human-Inspired". No venue is listed. Authors are from BAAI, Beijing Institute of Technology, Tsinghua and XYZ Embodied AI. — [arXiv abs page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26280); [v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v1)
- Platform: Unitree G1 humanoid with 29 DoF (12 legs, 3 waist, 14 arms), 1.32 m tall and 35 kg. Three PPO actor-critics (lower body, waist, upper body) run at 50 Hz and output joint position targets for a PD controller at 500 Hz. There are Dex3-1 hands, and VR teleoperation is retargeted with GMR. — [v4 full text, Sec. III–IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- FAT2 method: a sagittal-plane quasi-static moment balance about the midpoint of the feet (p₀) gives a reference horizontal CoM offset d* = −M_h(p₀)/(mg). M_h is the hand-contact pitch moment from hand forces and their moment arms. The reward is r = exp(−(d − d*)²/0.0225) with weight 2.0, applied to the lower-body policy as a soft reward and not as a constraint. Training uses a two-stage curriculum of end-effector force disturbances with Gaussian-randomized magnitude and direction. — [v4 full text, Sec. III-B, Table IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Arm joint-torque saturation: not discussed anywhere in v4. The only torque term is a generic joint-torque L2 regulariser (weight −10⁻⁵) listed among the "common regularizers (per policy)". FAT2 models the robot as a rigid body balanced on its feet; joint torque limits do not appear in the derivation. The limitations mention "actuator thermal limits" only as future work. — [v4 full text, Sec. III-B, V, Table IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Measured forces (v4): mean peak dual-hand pulling force was 167.7 ± 2.4 N walking backward and 145.5 ± 2.0 N walking forward. The best baseline, FALCON, reached 99.3 ± 1.3 N and 83.3 ± 2.7 N (+68.9% / +74.7%). Single-hand backward was 147.5 ± 4.9 N against FALCON's 92.4 ± 6.2 N. Dual-hand standing at 135° was 127.4 ± 3.3 N. — [v4 full text, Sec. IV-B, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Measurement protocol (v4): the rope was horizontal at hand height, with "20 trials" per method per condition. The peak force was "extracted … from a continuous 10 s interval of stable pulling, excluding transient peaks". Results are mean ± SE. v4 does not name the instrument; v1 says each data point is "the peak pulling force recorded by the dynamometer during a 10-second static measurement". — [v4 full text, Sec. IV-B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4); [v1 full text, Sec. IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v1)
- Body-posture-off ablation on the same hardware task: Thor2 (three policies, no FAT2) reached 104.6 ± 4.6 N backward and 103.6 ± 3.9 N forward. Adding FAT2 raised these by 60.3% and 40.4%. Thor1 (FAT2, two policies) reached 138.4 ± 5.4 N and 128.0 ± 3.5 N. — [v4 full text, Sec. IV-B, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Baselines: FALCON, HEFT, SONIC and Homie, all with official checkpoints. Task success over 20 trials per task: fire door 85% for Thor against 60% (FALCON), 15% (HEFT) and 0% (SONIC); car towing 80% against 0% for all baselines. The fire door needed "over 60 N with one hand". The car was 1.7 t, towed over 5 m at about 160 N. — [v4 full text, Table III, Sec. IV-B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Explanation offered (v1 only): pulling backward is stronger because "the frictional force from the ground is transmitted from the lower body to the waist and then to the upper body. By leaning backward, the robot further leverages its own body weight". v1 also says custom shoe covers were made because "ground friction was sometimes insufficient". — [v1 full text, Sec. IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v1)
- Stated limitations (v4): only short-duration tasks were tested. "Sustained material handling under varying ground friction and interaction loads remains to be systematically evaluated." — [v4 full text, Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)

#### 4. Zhang, Sorokin, Brüdigam, Hung, … Manchester, Le Cléac'h, "Sumo: Dynamic and Generalizable Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation" (arXiv 2604.08508, v3 read)
- Exists: arXiv 2604.08508. v1 was 9 Apr 2026 and v3 9 Sep 2026. The header reads "Preprint. Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Robot Learning". Authors are from MIT, the RAI Institute and Cornell. — [arXiv abs page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08508); [v3 full text header](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3)
- Platform: a Boston Dynamics Spot running the pre-trained "Relic" whole-body RL policy (50 Hz; it can stand on three legs and use the fourth as a manipulator). Above it, a CEM sampling MPC (32 rollouts, 1.5 s horizon, 20 Hz) plans in the policy's command space (base velocity, 6 arm joint targets, and optionally torso, leg and gripper). G1 tasks are in simulation only. — [full text, Sec. III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3)
- Arm rating: "a tire of 15kg, which is heavier than the maximum lifting capacity of 11kg of the Spot arm … The robot solves the task using a combination of its arm, torso, and legs." The other hardware objects were a 16 kg crowd barrier (uprighted and dragged as 16/15 kg), a 16.5 kg chair, a 15 kg tire stack, a 10 kg tire rack and a 20 kg box (pushed). — [full text, Sec. V-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3)
- How force or torque enters: it does not. The costs are object pose or goal terms, end-effector proximity, torso standoff, action regularisation, a fall penalty and a grasp-detection term based on gripper closure error. There are no force, torque or capability terms. — [full text, Sec. IV-A and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3)
- Measured forces: none. Results are success rates and completion times over 10 hardware trials per task (e.g. tire upright 10/10 in 9.2 ± 4.7 s; box push 10/10). State came from motion capture at 120 Hz fused with 333 Hz encoders, and planning ran off-board. — [full text, Table II, Sec. V-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3)
- Baselines: end-to-end RL, end-to-end MPC and hierarchical RL, in simulation only, on Move and Upright tasks with 20 evaluation trials. There is no arm-only or body-off baseline on any task. — [full text, Sec. IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3)
- Stated limitations: off-board estimation and planning, the sim-to-real gap and object-model building ("remains an art"), and no human priors. — [full text, Sec. VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3)

#### 5a. Zeng, Wang, Zhang, Zhang, Xu, Xia, Li, Liu, "Force-Aware Reinforcement Learning with Hybrid Sensorless Force Estimation for Wheeled-Legged Loco-Manipulation" (arXiv 2609.13779, v1 read)
- Exists: arXiv 2609.13779, v1 12 Sep 2026, 12 pages. No venue is listed. Authors are from CUHK. — [arXiv abs page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13779)
- Platform: "our wheeled-legged platform" with "the mounted 6-DoF arm". The robot and arm are not named in the text. The policy outputs a 22-D action (leg and arm joint position targets plus wheel velocities) at 50 Hz, with PD gains Kp = 40, Kd = 2 for the legs and Kp = 15, Kd = 1 for the arm. No arm torque or payload rating is given. — [full text, Sec. II-E, IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)
- Method: a generalized momentum observer gives a whole-body disturbance. A contact-constrained QP (friction-cone, unilateral) splits it into support reactions and the end-effector wrench, and a causal TCN residual corrects the remaining bias. The corrected force is a policy observation, and an axis-wise force/position selector allows free, force and hybrid modes. Training forces are a virtual spring-damper with commanded components in [−10, 10] N. — [full text, Sec. II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)
- How torque limits enter: only "torque-related terms" in the locomotion reward. There is no arm torque-limit or force-capability analysis. — [full text, Sec. II-E](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)
- Hardware measurements: an end-effector F/T sensor serves "only as an external quantitative reference", never as input. Commands were about 18 N for the valve, 15 N for wiping and up to about 30 N for the door, beyond the ±10 N training range. For a representative valve trial in place, estimation RMSE was 4.91 / 4.12 N and control RMSE 3.99 / 3.15 N (y/z); during locomotion these rose to 6.47 / 6.38 N and 5.82 / 5.42 N. Wiping normal-force RMSE was 2.13 N against a 15 N command, and estimate-versus-F/T error was 2.74 N. — [full text, Sec. IV-A, IV-B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)
- Door: a "spring-loaded door" whose spring "produces a restoring torque that opposes opening". A finite-state machine sequences grasp, press handle, push and redirect the force. The result is qualitative: no door torque or force, success rate or trial count is reported. The paper says "door closer" nowhere; that wording belongs to 2609.03889. — [full text, Sec. IV-C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)
- Baselines: Portela et al. and Zhi et al. were reproduced in simulation on a box-drag task over 10 trials. f_y RMSE was 0.98 N against 6.21 / 5.42 N, and f_z RMSE 3.98 N against 12.69 / 5.44 N. Estimator ablations (GMO-PI, GMO-CQP, TCN) are in simulation. There is no arm-only or posture-off baseline. — [full text, Sec. III, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)
- Stated limitations: dependence on robot-model fidelity, state and contact estimates, and coverage of the simulation data used for the residual estimator. — [full text, Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)

#### 5b. Zhang, Ma, Yang, Li, Hao, Chi, Wei, Yu, Hou, "FWBC-VLA: Force-Aware Whole-Body Compensation for Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation" (arXiv 2609.03889, v2 read)
- Exists: arXiv 2609.03889. v1 was 3 Sep 2026 and v2 4 Sep 2026, 9 pages. No venue is listed. Affiliations include Zhejiang University, Shanghai AI Laboratory, Tsinghua and Deep Robotics. — [arXiv abs page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03889)
- Platform: a DeepRobotics M20S wheeled-legged quadruped "equipped with a CM1 6-DoF robotic arm and a 1-DoF gripper", with three RealSense D435i cameras. No arm torque or payload figures are given. — [full text, Sec. "Experiments in the Real World"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Method: HSR-Force uses dual LSTM "experts" with a fixed gate to predict free-motion arm joint torque at 200 Hz, and treats measured minus predicted torque as an interaction residual. It is "a latent interaction variable rather than a directly calibrated force measurement". The residual feeds two paths. First, force tokens go into a fine-tuned π0.5 action expert at 15 Hz. Second, the residual is projected through Jacobians to an end-effector wrench and a body-frame wrench "including the lever-arm moment". A small MLP turns this into a bounded base-velocity residual (Δvx, Δvy, Δωz) for the WBC. — [full text, Method](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- How torque limits enter: they do not. There is no capability metric or torque-limit term. — [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Door: "evaluated with and without a door closer ( 50 N of force to push at the handle)". A leading symbol, probably "≈" or "∼", was lost in text extraction. No door torque in N·m is given. — [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Force measurements: estimator calibration with 0.36 kg and 0.72 kg loads "produced by a force gauge" (ground truth 3.53 N and 7.06 N). Real F/T sensor force is used for the ACP, ForceVLA and FWBC-GT baselines. — [full text, Dataset and Force-Estimator Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Results: 25 trials per task, scored as stage success rates. Door opening's final stage was 52% for FWBC-VLA, against 12% for ForceVLA and 48% for FWBC-GT (with the F/T sensor). Whiteboard wiping's final stage was 64%. — [full text, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Body-compensation ablation: on "push with closer" success went from 0% (no force) to 0% (force interface only) to 52% (force interface plus body compensation). Pressing the handle and pushing without the closer barely changed when body compensation was added. — [full text, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Internal inconsistency: the text says body compensation raises the average from 35.0% to 59.5% (+24.5 points), but Table 3 prints 65 for FWBC-VLA. The four printed stage values (60, 72, 52, 76) average to 65. — [full text, Table 3 and text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Stated limitation: "the impact of force estimation accuracy on task performance warrants further investigation". The text refers to an Appendix for the Jacobian projection details, but no appendix is in the arXiv PDF. — [full text, Conclusion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)

#### 6. Ferrolho, Ivan, Merkt, Havoutis, Vijayakumar, "RoLoMa: Robust Loco-Manipulation for Quadruped Robots with Arms" (arXiv 2203.01446, v2 read)
- Venue confirmed: Autonomous Robots 47(8):1463–1481, published 15 Oct 2023, DOI 10.1007/s10514-023-10146-0. — [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007/s10514-023-10146-0); [Springer page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-023-10146-0). The arXiv v2 (2 Oct 2023) was read, not the Springer typeset version. — [arXiv v2 full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Platform: ANYmal B quadruped with a Kinova arm (6-DoF; the exact Kinova model and its torque limits are not given in the text). There are 18 actuated joints. The base controller tracks feedforward torque plus PD at 400 Hz; the arm uses Kinova's velocity controller. Vicon gives the robot-to-target transform. — [full text, Sec. III-A, IV-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Method: offline direct-transcription trajectory optimization with full rigid-body dynamics (Julia, RigidBodyDynamics.jl, Knitro), using a prescribed contact sequence. Joint torque bounds τL ≤ τ ≤ τU apply to all limbs, and friction pyramids to the feet. The "smallest unrejectable force" (SUF) is the largest end-effector force the robot can resist from its worst direction, given foot friction and actuation limits. It is computed with gain matrices K_λ and K_τ and maximized as the objective (NLP Problem 3). Solve times range from 1.7 s to about 38 s, so the method is offline. — [full text, Sec. III, Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Lever and wheel results: these SUF magnitudes are computed by the planner and are not measured forces. For the lever pull the proposed trajectory gave 68–75 N against a baseline (torque and contact-force minimization) of 50–69 N, a mean increase of about 18%. For the hand wheel it gave 73–76 N against 56–66 N (about 24%). "Both the baseline and proposed trajectories successfully completed the task." The experiment was repeated "multiple times for different robot orientations" with "identical" results; no count is given. — [full text, Sec. IV-C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Binding constraint not identified: K_λ and K_τ "can be used to understand and explain the specific constraint that determines the upper bound of the SUF (i.e., friction cone or torque, on which foot or motor), although that is something we do not investigate in this paper." — [full text, Sec. III-D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Arm-only versus whole-body comparison on hardware: this is in the foot-placement section. "Nominal" keeps the base at its default joint positions with only the arm moving. "Baseline" re-postures the whole body and minimizes effort. "Proposed" maximizes SUF, with foot positions optimized in all three. Predicted SUF was 58 N, 57 N and 76 N respectively. — [full text, Sec. V, Fig. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Measured on hardware with a force gauge: the end effector was pulled from three angles for each trajectory, and the peak gauge reading was taken from video. The worst-case measured SUF was 60 N for Proposed, "approximately 33 % better than both nominal and baseline". Measured values were 21–22% below predicted. Robots slipped, lost foot contact or toppled, held by a harness. — [full text, Sec. V, Fig. 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Other hardware results: a 30 min harness-free repeatability test turning a hand wheel. In the bucket lift, the robot steps back while pulling a rope and lifted 1.1, 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1 kg; it failed at 5.1 kg when the handle slipped from the gripper. The payload was not modelled. — [full text, Sec. IV-B, IV-F](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Per-leg analysis: setting one leg's torques to their limits and its foot to the friction-cone boundary reduces the SUF, most for the hind-right leg in a pick-up motion. No equivalent analysis is done for the arm. — [full text, Sec. IV-D, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Stated limitations and future work: object dynamics are not modelled, the method is too slow for MPC, and contact sequences and timings must be given in advance. Future work includes force feedback during execution and computing the SUF "at the knees, at the elbows" to find bottlenecks along the kinematic chain. — [full text, Sec. VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)

### Inferences
- The brief's summaries are accurate as headlines, but three need correcting before they are cited in a thesis. (a) Portela's 90 N against 36 N compares a peak pull with an arm payload rating, not with an arm-only pull measured on the same setup. (b) RoLoMa's lever numbers are planner-computed worst-direction disturbance margins during the lever motion, not the force applied to the lever. (c) Sumo's "beyond the 11 kg lift" applies explicitly only to uprighting a 15 kg tire, which pivots on the ground, so the arm never carries the full weight.
- FORTE models exactly the student's series case: all force passes through the arm (P = {f : τ̃⁻ ≤ Jᵀf ≤ τ̃⁺}) and the base only changes J. Its gains are therefore posture-through-the-arm gains and cannot show amplification beyond the arm's torque envelope.

### Gaps
- Portela: the instrument, direction and trial count for the 90 N pull are not in the paper, and the project-page supplement was unreachable (404).
- 2609.13779: the robot and arm models are not named. The 22-D action is consistent with 12 leg joints, 4 wheels and 6 arm joints, but that is my inference.
- FORTE: numerical directional-capacity values exist only in figures and were not extracted.
- RoLoMa: the mapping of Fig. 11 per-angle labels to trajectories could not be recovered from the extracted text. Only the worst cases stated in the body (60 N against about 45 N) are reliable.
- Venues: FORTE, Thor, 2609.13779 and FWBC-VLA list none. Sumo states only "submitted to IEEE Transactions on Robot Learning".

## Q2. Does any paper quantify how much of the applied force the body contributes versus the arm, and explain why?

### Takeaway
No paper splits the applied force into a body share and an arm share. The nearest are on/off comparisons. Thor's FAT2 ablation measured +40–60% pulling force from a load-dependent CoM lean, explained by moment balance at the feet. RoLoMa compares arm-only against whole-body worst-case disturbance resistance (58 → 76 N predicted, about 45 → 60 N measured, roughly 1.3x). FWBC-VLA's base-compensation ablation shows success on pushing against a door closer rising from 0% to 52%. All three explain the gain by balance or friction at the feet, never by a parallel load path that bypasses the arm.

### Cited Findings
- Portela gives a single number (90 N) against the arm's 36 N rated payload. Its explanation is only that the policy "can coordinate the body with the legs to increase the applied force". There is no decomposition and no arm-only pull. — [Portela full text, Sec. V-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Thor: removing FAT2 (Thor2) lowers dual-hand pulling from 167.7 to 104.6 N backward and from 145.5 to 103.6 N forward, on the same task with 20 trials each. — [Thor v4 full text, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Thor's explanation is ZMP and moment balance. The CoM offset reference d* = −M_h(p₀)/(mg) keeps the ZMP at the foot midpoint against the hand-force moment. In simulation, "at high loads, the ground projection of its CoM lies outside the foot-support polygon." — [Thor v4 full text, Sec. III-B, IV-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Thor v1 adds that ground friction is "transmitted from the lower body to the waist and then to the upper body. By leaning backward, the robot further leverages its own body weight". Single-hand pulling is weaker because "the robot must overcome its own torsional moment". — [Thor v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v1)
- RoLoMa: SUF with the base fixed at default (arm-only reach) was 58 N predicted, against 76 N with the body re-postured to maximize SUF. Measured worst cases were about 45 N and 60 N, "approximately 33 % better". — [RoLoMa full text, Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- RoLoMa explains the gain qualitatively: the optimized pose widens the support polygon and places the CoM away from its centre because the metric "takes into account the robot capabilities". It explicitly does not identify which constraint (friction or torque, which foot or motor) binds. — [RoLoMa full text, Sec. III-D, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- FWBC-VLA: adding body compensation (bounded base-velocity residuals from the body-frame load proxy) lifts "push with closer" stage success from 0% to 52%. The authors attribute this to "maintaining stable whole-body behavior under sustained external forces", not to added force capacity. — [FWBC-VLA full text, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- FORTE: the base contributes no force by construction ("regarded as a kinematic redundancy source"). Gains come only from moving the arm into configurations whose Jacobian maps the residual torques to larger forces in the task direction. — [FORTE full text, Sec. 3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Sumo and 2609.13779 report no force decomposition and no body-versus-arm comparison. — [Sumo full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3); [2609.13779 full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)

### Inferences
- The measured "body on versus off" ratios in this literature are about 1.3x (RoLoMa: 76/58 predicted, 60/45 measured) and 1.4–1.6x (Thor FAT2). Both sit near the student's static estimate of about 1.3x for crouching with a rigid arm. They are consistent with the student's claim that re-posturing the body while the load still runs through the arm buys tens of percent, not multiples. Only Portela's 2.5x (90/36) is larger, and it compares against a payload rating at an unstated posture rather than an arm-only pull.
- In Thor's rope pull the rope is horizontal at hand height and the arms are presumably extended along it. That would be a near-singular "tie" posture in which arm joint torques stay small whatever the rope tension, which would explain why arm torque never shows up as the limit. This is my inference; Thor reports no arm posture or arm torques.
- None of the papers' explanations (ZMP, tipping, friction, stability) involves where the reaction force travels inside the robot. The series-versus-parallel load-path argument is therefore not made in this set.

### Gaps
- No paper reports arm joint torques during the force tests, so the arm's share of the load cannot be reconstructed from any of them.

## Q3. Does any paper show that the gain is capped by the arm's joint torques when the force passes through the arm?

### Takeaway
None shows this experimentally or states it as a result. FORTE and RoLoMa build arm torque limits into their capability metrics, and FORTE's metric uses the arm's limits alone. But FORTE never asks whether the body could add force beyond the arm's envelope, and RoLoMa explicitly declines to identify which constraint binds. Portela only penalizes arm torques above 80% of maximum with a tiny weight, and Thor does not discuss arm torque at all. The arm-torque cap remains an open, publishable point.

### Cited Findings
- FORTE's RFP is "computed solely from the arm actuator torque limits" (60 N·m and 12 N·m conservative limits). It notes that a configuration "with strong static force capability … may decrease significantly during high-speed or high-acceleration motions". It never frames the arm's limits as a cap on what the body can add, because the base is assumed to execute its motion perfectly. — [FORTE full text, Sec. 3.1–3.2, Eq. 38](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- RoLoMa constrains every limb's torques (τL ≤ τ ≤ τU) and the SUF limb inequality, but "the specific constraint that determines the upper bound of the SUF (i.e., friction cone or torque, on which foot or motor) … is something we do not investigate in this paper." — [RoLoMa full text, Sec. III-C, III-D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- RoLoMa's per-leg "limited leg" analysis shows SUF drops when one leg's motors are at their limits, but no arm-joint version is given. Its future work proposes computing the SUF "at the elbows" to find bottlenecks along the kinematic chain. — [RoLoMa full text, Sec. IV-D, VI-C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Portela: the arm torque-limit term is the indicator 1[τ_a > 0.8|τ_a^max|] with weight −0.0015. Torque saturation is not mentioned in the results, and the authors cannot say whether their accuracy limit is "a limit of the hardware". — [Portela full text, Table I, Sec. VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Thor: there is no arm-torque discussion, only a −10⁻⁵ L2 torque regulariser applied to each policy. Future work names "actuator thermal limits". — [Thor v4 full text, Table IV, Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Sumo, 2609.13779 and FWBC-VLA contain no arm torque-limit analysis. — [Sumo full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3); [2609.13779 full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1); [FWBC-VLA full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)

### Inferences
- FORTE's single-point holding results show the duality behind the student's "strut" idea without naming it. Force-maximizing configurations (RFP inscribed radius and RFP cone) "sacrifice Cartesian velocity manipulability in order to enlarge the force capability" (thinner ellipsoids). Large force capability along a direction coincides with low velocity capability along it, which is the near-singular case. FORTE treats this as a trade-off to balance, not as a deliberate strut posture.
- A paper showing that (i) for force paths through the arm, body posture changes saturate when the first arm joint hits its torque limit, and (ii) parallel paths (bracing, body contact, a fulcrumed tool) break that cap, would be new relative to this set. Building it on RoLoMa's K_τ readout or FORTE's per-joint signed distances would give a citable mechanism.

### Gaps
- The Springer version of RoLoMa was not read. The arXiv v2 statement that the binding constraint is not investigated may have been revised, though this seems unlikely given the published date matches v2.

## Q4. Which hardware force measurements and baselines do they use? (the bar a new paper must meet)

### Takeaway
The highest measurement bar in this set is Thor's: 20 trials per method per condition, mean ± SE, 11 pull conditions, four external baselines plus two ablations, and a dynamometer named in v1 only. RoLoMa is the only paper with an arm-only versus whole-body comparison measured by an external force gauge, but it uses a single pull per angle read from video. The wheeled-legged preprints use an end-effector F/T sensor as reference (2609.13779) or 25-trial stage success rates (FWBC-VLA). Sumo and FORTE measure no forces on hardware.

### Cited Findings
- Portela: a dynamometer measured commanded downward force (0–70 N) over 5 trials at 5 gripper positions, with tracking error 5–10 N. The 90 N pull's measurement method is not stated. — [Portela full text, Sec. V-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2)
- Thor: 20 trials per method per condition, the peak taken from 10 s of stable pulling with the rope horizontal at hand height, reported as mean ± SE. Baselines were FALCON, HEFT, SONIC and Homie, with two ablations; task success was over 20 trials per task. v1 names a dynamometer. — [Thor v4 full text, Sec. IV-B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4); [Thor v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v1)
- RoLoMa: a force gauge between the end effector and the puller, three angles per trajectory, with the peak read from video. Predicted and measured SUF are compared (measured 21–22% lower). The bucket lift used incremental 1 kg weights until failure. — [RoLoMa full text, Sec. IV-F, V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- 2609.13779: an end-effector F/T sensor serves as external reference only. RMSEs are reported for single representative valve and wiping trials; door opening is qualitative. Simulation baselines (Portela, Zhi) ran 10 trials each. — [2609.13779 full text, Sec. III-D, IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1)
- FWBC-VLA: force-gauge loads (3.53 / 7.06 N) for estimator calibration and 25 trials per task. Baselines are OpenVLA, StarVLA, π0.5, GR00T N1.6, ACP and ForceVLA, plus FWBC-GT with a real F/T sensor. — [FWBC-VLA full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- FORTE: model-computed RFP capability with 3 hardware trials per method; task failure (losing the lift) is the only physical outcome. — [FORTE full text, Sec. 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Sumo: 10 hardware trials per task, scored by success and time, with no force data. — [Sumo full text, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3)

### Inferences
- A new paper making a force-amplification claim on a Go2 + D1 would sit above most of this set if it measured lever or door torque (N·m) or handle force with an inline load cell or force gauge. It should run at least about 10–20 trials per condition, report mean ± SE, and include, on the same task: (a) arm only with the body fixed, (b) body re-posturing with the load still through the arm (the series case), and (c) a parallel path. It should also report arm joint torque or current at the peak. Thor's protocol (peak over a stable window, rope geometry fixed) is the clearest template. RoLoMa's predicted-versus-measured comparison is a good model for validating a static analysis.

### Gaps
- None of the papers reports measurement uncertainty or calibration of its force instrument.

## Q5. What of the student's angle do these papers already cover, and what remains open?

### Takeaway
Already covered: legged or whole-body posture raising end-effector pull force on hardware (Portela, Thor), posture optimization with torque-limit-aware force-capability metrics (RoLoMa's SUF, FORTE's task-oriented RFP signed distance), and sensorless force-aware wheeled-legged door opening against a spring or closer (2609.13779, FWBC-VLA). Still open: arm-only versus body versus parallel-path comparisons on a lever or door, measured in N·m; the series-versus-parallel load-path explanation; showing the arm-torque cap; deliberate near-singular strut postures; and a weak (few N·m), position-commanded, about 10 Hz servo arm.

### Cited Findings
- Arm-only versus body-posture comparison on the same task: partly covered. RoLoMa compares base-fixed arm-only against whole-body for disturbance resistance at a held pose (force gauge). Thor compares FAT2 on/off for rope pulling. Neither uses a lever or door. — [RoLoMa full text, Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2); [Thor v4 full text, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4)
- Series-versus-parallel load path: not covered. Explanations are ZMP and moment balance (Thor), support polygon and capability (RoLoMa), arm Jacobian only (FORTE) and stability compensation (FWBC-VLA). — [Thor v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4); [RoLoMa](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2); [FORTE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1); [FWBC-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Near-singular strut postures: not discussed as a strategy. FORTE optimizes manipulability as an objective that competes with force capability. — [FORTE full text, Sec. 4.3, 5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1)
- Weak, position-controlled servo arm: not covered at the D1's scale. The arms used are a Unitree Z1 (36 N rated payload, PD position targets via RL), a Franka (torque-controlled, 60 / 12 N·m conservative limits), the Spot arm (11 kg lift), the G1 arms, a Kinova on ANYmal, an unnamed 6-DoF arm with PD position targets at Kp = 15, and a CM1. None reports few-N·m joints or about 10 Hz position-only commands. — [Portela](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01402v2); [FORTE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21497v1); [Sumo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08508v3); [RoLoMa](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2); [2609.13779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1); [FWBC-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2)
- Lever or door torque in N·m: not reported anywhere. The nearest are FWBC-VLA's door closer at about 50 N at the handle, Thor's fire door at over 60 N one-handed, 2609.13779's door commands up to about 30 N (spring torque unquantified), and RoLoMa's lever (applied force not reported). — [FWBC-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03889v2); [Thor v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26280v4); [2609.13779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13779v1); [RoLoMa](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446v2)
- Adjacent papers seen while working but not assigned and not read in full (abstract only, unverified): "Brace Yourself: Task-Conditioned Environmental Bracing for Forceful Humanoid Manipulation" (Zhang, Lehnert, Browne, Roberts; arXiv 2609.25486, 21 Sep 2026, submitted to RA-L). Its abstract reports a G1 bracing with one hand reaching "usable contact forces up to 60 N, compared with a maximum sustained force of 13.5 N without environmental bracing". — [arXiv abs page (abstract only)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25486)
- Also abstract only: "Contact as a Decision Variable: Capability-Tradeoff Contact Selection for Legged Loco-Manipulation" (Mahmud, Li, Wang; arXiv 2609.30140, 24 Sep 2026). It selects environmental support contacts by residual wrench, reach and mobility on "a Unitree Go2 quadruped with an AgileX NERO arm" across 392 task conditions and nine support surfaces, and reports beating ground-only and fixed-contact support. — [arXiv abs page (abstract only)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30140)

### Inferences
- What is left for the student's paper is the mechanism, not "body posture increases force", which is published. The novel pieces are: (1) showing on one lever or door task, in N·m, that series re-posturing saturates near the student's 1.3x because of the D1's shoulder and elbow torque limits; (2) showing that parallel paths (bracing, body contact, a fulcrum or body-mounted tool, strut postures) break that cap; (3) doing it with a low-cost position-commanded servo arm where the cap binds early.
- The two September 2026 bracing and contact-selection preprints (2609.25486, 2609.30140) are the most direct threat to novelty for the "parallel path" half. 2609.30140 uses the student's own quadruped, the Go2. They should be read in full before claiming novelty for bracing.

### Gaps
- Not checked, because it is outside the assigned list: whether these groups have follow-up work that adds an arm-only force baseline. One candidate is the unified force/position legged-manipulation policy that 2609.13779 reproduces as "Zhi et al." ([13]). A search result suggests it is arXiv 2505.20829, "Learning a Unified Policy for Position and Force Control in Legged Loco-Manipulation", but this was not opened.
