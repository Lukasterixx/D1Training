# Force capability theory: how posture, configuration, bracing and leverage set the maximum force a manipulator or whole-body robot can exert

Scope note: these notes cover the analysis tools and prior results. Tags: **[F]** = foundational (pre-2010); **[R]** = recent (2020-2026). Equations quoted from a source are marked as such. Where I derive something from a cited definition, it is under "Inferences". Project context (Go2 ~15 kg with a welded Unitree D1 6-DoF servo arm; static analysis gives median 0.94 N·m and best 1.5 N·m lever torque when crouching with a rigid arm, ~1.3x over the arm alone) comes from the assignment brief and was not re-checked here.

## 1. Force capability metrics: which are standard, what are their limits, and which handle joint torque limits exactly?

### Takeaway
The standard tools are the manipulability and force ellipsoids (Yoshikawa 1985) and their task-directed version, the "transmission ratio" or task compatibility index (Chiu 1988). Both treat torque limits only approximately: the torque box is replaced by a ball, there is no gravity bias, and units are mixed. The force (wrench) polytope (Chiacchio et al. 1997; Sasaki; Skuric et al. 2021) is exact for linear joint-torque bounds with gravity and asymmetric limits. Polytopes also compose correctly: a Minkowski sum when two chains push in parallel, an intersection when one force must pass through two chains in series. Legged-robot versions exist: the actuation and feasible wrench polytopes (AWP/FWP) of Orsolino et al. and the residual force polytope / smallest unrejectable force of Ferrolho et al. For a single required direction (the lever's tangent), the exact polytope answer is a small closed-form or linear-programming problem, so no vertex enumeration is needed.

### Cited Findings
**Basic statics and the ellipsoid family [F]**
- Statics: τ = Jᵀ(q) F_tip, so to produce a tip force the motors must supply τ = JᵀF. Joint torque limits map to a configuration-dependent end-effector "force ellipse or ellipsoid", reciprocal to the velocity (manipulability) ellipsoid. — [Lynch & Park, Modern Robotics, Ch. 5](https://modernrobotics.northwestern.edu/nu-gm-book-resource/velocity-kinematics-and-statics/)
- Yoshikawa's manipulability measure: w = √det(J Jᵀ) = s₁s₂…s_n, which is proportional to the ellipsoid volume and measures distance from singularity. The inverse condition number c = s_n/s₁ = 1/cond(J) is the usual isotropy index. A joint-limit penalty P(θ) = 1 − exp(−k Πⱼ (θⱼ−lⱼ⁻)(lⱼ⁺−θⱼ)/(lⱼ⁺−lⱼ⁻)²) is often multiplied in. — [Vahrenkamp et al., "Manipulability Analysis", Humanoids 2012](https://h2t.iar.kit.edu/pdf/Vahrenkamp2012c.pdf); original: [Yoshikawa, "Manipulability of Robotic Mechanisms", IJRR 4(2), 1985](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Manipulability-of-Robotic-Mechanisms-Yoshikawa/9d5085f3008a60abd7e53bc7f16963c4db32e342) **[F]**
- Velocity and force manipulability ellipsoids were introduced by Yoshikawa (1985) as kinetostatic measures of the preferred directions for force or velocity at a configuration. Dynamic manipulability (Yoshikawa, ICRA 1985) measures the end-effector acceleration achievable per unit joint torque and matters when dynamics cannot be neglected. — [Jaquier et al., Geometry-aware Manipulability Learning (review section)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.11050) **[F origin]**
- Torque-weighted definitions as implemented in pycapacity: force ellipsoid E_f = {f | τ = Jᵀf, ‖W⁻¹τ‖ ≤ 1}, W = diag(τ_max); acceleration (dynamic) ellipsoid E_a = {ẍ | ẍ = J M⁻¹ τ, ‖W⁻¹τ‖ ≤ 1}. — [pycapacity.robot docs](https://auctus-team.gitlabpages.inria.fr/people/antunskuric/pycapacity/pycapacity.robot.html)
- Task compatibility (Chiu 1988): the manipulator is treated as a "mechanical transformer" from joint velocity and force to task velocity and force. The index rates postures by how well their velocity and force transmission ratios match the task's requirements along chosen directions, and is used for redundancy resolution and workspace design. — [Chiu, "Task Compatibility of Manipulator Postures", IJRR 7(5):13-21, 1988](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498800700502) **[F]**. Later variants include direction-specific and torque-weighted versions, and a transmission ratio defined as "the distance to the wrench ellipsoid from the origin in a desired direction". — [Marais, Pizarro & Williams, Frontiers in Robotics & AI 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2024.1442813/full) **[R]**
- Bowling & Khatib's Dynamic Capability Equations combine end-effector acceleration, velocity and force capability. They give the translational and rotational acceleration and force magnitudes "guaranteed to be achievable in every direction" under motor torque limits, and treat the unit mismatch between translation and rotation explicitly. — [Bowling & Khatib, IEEE T-RO 21(1), 2005](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/groups/manips/publications/pdfs/Bowling_2005_TransRA.pdf) **[F]**

**Known limitations of ellipsoids**
- Ellipsoids "largely approximate and underestimate the true capabilities" and can lead to "a drastic underestimation of the real kineto-static capacities of robots". Sums and intersections of ellipsoids (needed for several robots or limbs) are hard to compute and interpret, whereas these operations are well defined for polytopes. — [Skuric, Padois & Daney, ICRA 2021 (arXiv 2011.05226)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.05226) **[R]**
- Force ellipsoids "can be imprecise due to the fact that... the actuators' torque limits are not taken into account". Robot mass and payload translate the force ellipsoid. For compliant robots the force boundary is "nonpolytopic in general", because load changes the configuration. — [Ajoudani, Tsagarakis & Bicchi, "Choosing Poses for Force and Stiffness Control"](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/polytopes_stiffness.pdf)
- Units: for robots with translational and rotational DoF, the matrices behind the condition number "are not homogeneous in terms of units", so the index changes with the choice of units. — [Merlet, "Jacobian, manipulability, condition number and accuracy of parallel robots" (ISRR 2005; J. Mech. Design 2006)](https://www-sop.inria.fr/hephaistos/PDF/merlet_isrr2005.pdf) **[F]**. Ellipsoids that mix translation and rotation cause problems that polytopes avoid at higher computational cost. — [Vahrenkamp et al. 2012](https://h2t.iar.kit.edu/pdf/Vahrenkamp2012c.pdf)
- Human validation: Rezzoug, Hernandez & Gorce predicted measured upper-limb isometric force sets from posture and maximum joint torques with four models (force ellipsoid, scaled force ellipsoid, force polytope, scaled force polytope). Over the force-ellipsoid surface, 39.7% of points had error < 50 N, 33.6% had 50-100 N and 26.8% had > 100 N. — [Rezzoug, Hernandez & Gorce, Biomechanics 1(1):102-117, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomechanics1010008) **[R]**. A search-engine summary of the same comparison reports 56.2% of polytope points within 50 N, but I could not open the full text to confirm it.

**Polytopes: exact handling of torque limits [F origin, R tools]**
- Chiacchio et al. showed that the exact wrench and twist capacities are polytopes. Polytopes do not underestimate capacity and can include asymmetric limits, dynamics and external forces. — [Skuric et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.05226), citing [Chiacchio, Bouffard-Vercelli & Pierrot, "Force polytope and force ellipsoid for redundant manipulators", J. Robotic Systems 1997](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Force-polytope-and-force-ellipsoid-for-redundant-Chiacchio-Bouffard-Vercelli/13c41f39ff345c63144635ef1fe77b3564d3a712) **[F]**
- Definition (quoted from Skuric et al., eq. 2 and 5): Jᵀf + τ₀ = τ with τ₀ ∈ Ker(J); P_f = { f ∈ ℝᵐ | τ ∈ [τ_min, τ_max] ∩ Im(Jᵀ), Jᵀ f = τ }. The torque box is an n-dimensional parallelotope. Following Sasaki et al., force-polytope vertices lie on its (n−m)-dimensional faces and f_vert = (Jᵀ)⁺ τ_vert. Their algorithm computes vertices for 6- and 7-DoF robots in under 3 ms in Python. — [Skuric et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.05226)
- Gravity enters as a torque bias. pycapacity defines P_f = {f | τ = Jᵀf, τ_min ≤ τ ≤ τ_max} with an optional `t_bias`. It also provides the **sum** P = {f | f = f₁+f₂, τ₁ = J₁ᵀf₁, τ₂ = J₂ᵀf₂, limits on τ₁, τ₂} and the **intersection** P = {f | f₁ = f₂ = f, τ₁ = J₁ᵀf₁, τ₂ = J₂ᵀf₂, limits}, using the `vertex_enumeration_vepoli2` algorithm. — [pycapacity.robot docs](https://auctus-team.gitlabpages.inria.fr/people/antunskuric/pycapacity/pycapacity.robot.html); package: [GitHub auctus-team/pycapacity](https://github.com/auctus-team/pycapacity) (pip-installable; works with robotics-toolbox, Pinocchio, OpenSim and biorbd) **[R]**
- Vertex enumeration is "computationally intensive" and is "the main obstacle for wider uses of polytopes instead of ellipsoids". — [Skuric et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.05226). Explicit descriptions of projected polytopes are NP-hard in general. — [Ferrolho et al., RoLoMa, Autonomous Robots 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446)

**Legged and whole-body extensions**
- Actuation Wrench Polytope (quoted from Orsolino et al., eqs. 1-3). For each limb, f_i^max = J_i^{T#}(J_iᵀ M_biᵀ q̈_b + M_i q̈_i + c_i + g_i − τ_i^max). The 2ⁿ torque-limit combinations give the vertices of a 3D foot-force polytope. Each vertex is lifted to 6D as w = [f; p_k × f], and the 6D limb polytopes are combined by Minkowski sum: AWP = W₁ ⊕ W₂ ⊕ … ⊕ W_nb. The Feasible Wrench Polytope is FWP = AWP ∩ CWC (contact wrench cone: unilaterality and friction). It contains the CoM wrenches allowed by both posture and actuation and by the contacts. — [Orsolino, Focchi, Mastalli, Dai, Caldwell & Semini, "The AWP and the FWP" (arXiv 1712.02731)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.02731); applied in [Orsolino et al., RA-L 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.06833) and extended in the actuation-aware [Feasible Region (Orsolino et al.)](https://iit-dlslab.github.io/papers/orsolino19arxiv.pdf) **[F/R boundary: 2017-2020]**
- The residual force polytope counts only the torque left over after a nominal trajectory's controls. It is an exact representation of the task-space forces the whole floating-base robot can still counteract, given its dynamics. — [Ferrolho et al., Robotics & Autonomous Systems 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921889021000993); [arXiv 1908.05380](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1908.05380v2) **[R]**
- The smallest unrejectable force (SUF) is "the smallest force magnitude (applied from any possible direction) that the robot is not able to counteract", i.e. the largest worst-case force. The floating-base quasi-static constraint is split into base rows, J_s,baseᵀ K_λ + J_e,baseᵀ ρ = 0 (equality), and limb rows, a_τᵀ(−J_s,limbsᵀ K_λ − J_e,limbsᵀ ρ) ≤ b_τ (torque limits), together with friction-cone constraints on K_λ. — [Ferrolho, Ivan, Merkt, Havoutis & Vijayakumar, RoLoMa (arXiv 2203.01446)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446) **[R]**
- FORTE (Sept 2026) defines the residual force polytope R = {f | τ̃⁻ ≤ J_vᵀ(q) f ≤ τ̃⁺} with τ̃⁺ = τ_max − τ_nom and τ̃⁻ = −τ_max − τ_nom, where τ_nom comes from inverse dynamics including gravity. Its task metric is a signed distance d = minᵢ [(α τ̃ᵢ − nᵢᵀ f_c)/‖nᵢ‖ − r] between a ball of task-force uncertainty and the polytope faces; d < 0 means the capability is insufficient. — [Wang, Zhang, Solak, Zhao & Ajoudani, FORTE (arXiv 2609.21497)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21497) **[R]**
- Contact wrench cone (CWC): the set of all feasible wrenches that contacts can apply to the robot. It replaces the ZMP for multi-contact feasibility, and closed-form formulas exist for rectangular feet (Caron, Pham & Nakamura, ICRA 2015). — summarised in [Caron et al., "Leveraging Cone Double Description for Multi-contact Stability"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312660911_Leveraging_Cone_Double_Description_for_Multi-contact_Stability_of_Humanoids_with_Applications_to_Statics_and_Dynamics) **[R-ish, 2015]**
- A recent penalty for torque saturation, the "torque utilisation" ρ_r = [ (1/|A_r|) Σⱼ (|τⱼ|/τⱼ^max)⁴ ]^{1/2}, heavily penalises the most-loaded joints. — [Zhang, Lehnert, Browne & Roberts, "Brace Yourself" (arXiv 2609.25486, Sept 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25486v1) **[R]**

### Inferences
- **Directional maximum force, exact and cheap (derived from the H-representation above).** For a required force direction u (unit) at the tool, with gravity bias b = g(q) and a = Jᵀu (the joint torque per newton along u), the largest feasible magnitude is s* = minᵢ { (τ_max,i − bᵢ)/aᵢ if aᵢ > 0; (τ_min,i − bᵢ)/aᵢ if aᵢ < 0 }. The minimising joint is the bottleneck joint. This is the polytope version of Chiu's force transmission ratio and is what the lever task needs. The ellipsoid analogue, from E_f with unit-weighted torques, is β(u) = (uᵀ J W⁻² Jᵀ u)^{-1/2}, which is generally smaller and ignores b. (My derivation; Chiu's exact summed-index formula is not quoted here because I could not access the 1988 text.)
- **Series load path = intersection; parallel load paths = Minkowski sum.** This is the key framing for the Go2 + D1 problem. When the lever reaction must pass through the arm and then through the legs to the ground, the feasible lever force is the arm polytope **intersected** with the body/legs feasible-wrench set (pycapacity's `force_polytope_intersection` semantics). However large the body's AWP/FWP, the result cannot exceed the arm's polytope in that direction. That matches the student's ~1.3x result: body weight only re-poses the arm (it changes J_arm and the gravity bias). Gains above the arm polytope require a **parallel** path whose capability is added (`force_polytope_sum` semantics): a brace contact, the body or a leg pushing the lever directly, or a fulcrum or tool reacting part of the load.
- **The arm-joint rows decouple from the legs.** In the RoLoMa/FORTE floating-base statics, the rows for the arm joints contain no foot-contact Jacobian terms when the feet are the only other contacts. So τ_arm = g_arm(q) − J_e,armᵀ w_e (sign convention aside), whatever the legs do. Whole-body posture can only (a) re-orient the arm relative to w_e, (b) change the arm's gravity loading, or (c) add contacts. This is a short formal statement of the student's hypothesis.
- **Ellipsoid results should not be quoted as capability for a weak servo arm.** The D1's limits are probably asymmetric in practice (gravity bias) and the task is direction-specific. Use the polytope directional maximum, and report which joint binds.

### Gaps
- I could not access Chiu (1988) or Chiacchio et al. (1997) in full text, so their exact index formulas are not quoted. The ellipsoid transmission ratio given above is derived from the pycapacity ellipsoid definition, not quoted from Chiu.
- I found no source that publishes the D1's true structural, gearbox or backdrive limits. Every polytope above assumes joint torque is the only limit (see Section 3 on why this fails near singularity).
- I did not verify Yoshikawa's 1985 dynamic manipulability equation from the primary text. The pycapacity E_a definition is a velocity-free simplification.

## 2. Posture and configuration optimisation for maximum force: arms, mobile manipulators, humanoids and legged manipulators

### Takeaway
Optimising posture for force is well established. For fixed arms, redundancy is chosen to match force transmission to the task (Chiu) or to maximise stiffness for machining. For mobile manipulators, base placement and redundancy are chosen to maximise directional force or wrench (Xing 2020; Tugal 2022; Marais 2022/2025; FORTE 2026). For humanoids, ZMP/CWC-aware whole-body pushing postures and multi-contact posture generation under torque limits (Harada 2003; Nozawa 2012; Murooka 2015; Bouyarmane & Kheddar; Sentis & Khatib) are the reference work. For legged manipulators, RoLoMa (ANYmal + Kinova, including a lever task) maximises worst-case resistible force, and learned whole-body force control exceeds an arm's rated payload (Portela 2024). Two very recent papers (Sept 2026) are close to the student's question: humanoid environmental bracing (4.4-4.9x force range) and contact selection on a **Unitree Go2** with an arm and a body-mounted support frame.

### Cited Findings
**Arms**
- Humans choose arm configuration rather than joint stiffness to get a desired endpoint impedance. Ajoudani et al. argue that geometry dominates interaction capability, because torque saturation under Cartesian stiffness control happens later in some configurations than others. They define "stiffness feasibility regions" under torque limits and choose poses that align this region with the expected disturbance and minimise the shift caused by gravity. Their linearised statics are δτ = Jᵀ(q₀)δf + (∂Jᵀf₀/∂q)δq + (∂τ_g/∂q)δq. — [Ajoudani, Tsagarakis & Bicchi](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/polytopes_stiffness.pdf)
- Machining practice optimises the redundant DoF of 5-axis milling (rotation about the tool axis) to maximise a stiffness performance index, subject to joint limits, singularity avoidance and smoothness. — [Posture and path optimization based on stiffness performance index in robotic milling, Int. J. Adv. Manuf. Tech. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-024-14109-2); earlier: [Posture optimization in robot-assisted machining operations, Mech. Mach. Theory 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0094114X11002357). A search summary reported stiffness varying by 58-161% across redundant angles at a fixed cutter location, but I could not tie that figure to a specific paper.

**Mobile manipulators: base placement and reconfiguration for force**
- Xing, Torabi, Ding, Gao, Deng & Tavakoli improve force-exertion capability and tracking together by kinematic reconfiguration of wheeled mobile manipulators. — [RA-L 5(4):5842-5849, 2020](https://researchr.org/publication/XingTDGDT20) **[R]**
- Tugal, Cetin, Petillot et al. optimise vehicle position with constrained particle-swarm optimisation to maximise the arm's **directional** manipulability ("effort transmission to a task in desired directions"). A forward-dynamics controller suppresses excessive motion near singular configurations. — [Autonomous Robots 46:769-782, 2022](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/manipulation-at-optimum-locations-for-maximum-force-transmission-/) **[R]**
- Marais, Williams & Pizarro pose wrench maximisation for kinematically redundant systems as a linear program inside a bi-level optimisation over the redundancy parameters. It covers multiple manipulators and contacts (load lifting, valve turning), with experiments on an underwater vehicle-manipulator system. — [arXiv 2202.13535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13535); [Frontiers Robotics & AI 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2024.1442813/full) **[R]**
- FORTE optimises whole-body redundancy (base and 7-DoF Franka on MOCA) for a task-adaptive force margin. It uses conservative limits of 60 N·m for joints 1-4 and 12 N·m for joints 5-7, treats the base as kinematic redundancy without wheel-actuation limits, and covers only translational forces. — [FORTE, arXiv 2609.21497](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21497) **[R]**

**Humanoids: whole-body pushing and multi-contact**
- Harada, Kajita, Kaneko & Hirukawa distinguish the ZMP from a "generalized ZMP" (GZMP) when the hands push an object, and plan stable pushing from the GZMP's stable region. — [Pushing manipulation by humanoid considering two kinds of ZMPs, 2003](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4041370_Pushing_manipulation_by_humanoid_considering_two-kinds_of_ZMPs) **[F]**
- Nozawa, Kakiuchi, Okada & Inaba add dual-arm reaction-force control to a full-body balancer and estimate object-floor friction, so that HRP-2 pushes a 90 kg wheelchair without slipping or falling. — [ICRA 2012](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6224884/) **[F/R boundary]**
- Murooka et al. plan whole-body pushing in which the contact points on the body (not only the hands) are chosen according to the required pushing force. They derive the statics for external forces on arbitrary body regions and push large, heavy objects with a real humanoid. — [ICRA 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283768584_Whole-body_pushing_manipulation_with_contact_posture_planning_of_large_and_heavy_object_for_humanoid_robot)
- Li & Nguyen's kinodynamic pose optimisation plus MPC lets a humanoid push objects up to 20 kg while resisting 120 N lateral disturbances. — [arXiv 2303.04985 (Humanoids 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04985) **[R]**
- Sentis, Park & Khatib model internal forces and CoM behaviour in multi-contact with a "virtual-linkage model" and a grasp/contact matrix, and integrate internal-force control into prioritised whole-body control (Asimo). — [IEEE T-RO 26(3):483-501, 2010](https://khatib.stanford.edu/publications/pdfs/Sentis_2010_TRO.pdf). Operational-space origin: [Khatib 1987](https://www.scribd.com/document/190806462/A-Unified-Approach-for-Motion-and-Force-Control-of-Robot-Manipulators-The-Operational-Space-Formulation-Oussama-Khatib) **[F]**. Consolidated: [Khatib, Jorda, Park, Sentis & Chung, IJRR 2022](https://dx.doi.org/10.1177/02783649221120029) **[R]**
- Bouyarmane & Kheddar generate multi-contact postures by nonlinear optimisation, with static stability, collision avoidance, **torque limits** and joint limits as constraints. — as summarised in [arXiv 2303.08232](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08232); see also [Humanoid robot locomotion and manipulation step planning](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254217701_Humanoid_Robot_Locomotion_and_Manipulation_Step_Planning) and the [Springer Humanoid Robotics handbook chapter "Multi-contact Motion Planning and Control"](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-94-007-6046-2_32)
- Ott's group: passivity-based whole-body balancing for torque-controlled humanoids in multi-contact (Henze, Roa & Ott, IJRR 2016), as cited in [Brace Yourself](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25486v1).

**Legged manipulators**
- RoLoMa (ANYmal B + Kinova) maximises the SUF in trajectory optimisation. For **pulling down a lever**, the SUF rose from 50-69 N (baseline: minimise torque and contact force) to 68-75 N, an ~18% mean increase. For turning a hand wheel it rose from 56-66 N to 73-76 N (~24%). — [RoLoMa, arXiv 2203.01446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446) **[R]**
- Portela, Margolis, Ji & Agrawal (Unitree B1 55 kg + Z1 arm) learn whole-body force control. Pulling down with the arm in front, they measured 90 N, against the arm's rated payload of 36 N. The whole-body controller enlarged the reachable workspace by 59% (0.81 m³ to 1.29 m³). — [Learning Force Control for Legged Manipulation, arXiv 2405.01402 (ICRA 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01402v1) **[R]**
- Mahmud, Li & Wang (Sept 2026) use a **Unitree Go2 with an AgileX NERO 7-DoF arm and a rigid support frame mounted on the body**. They treat environmental support contact as a decision variable and score candidates by residual wrench capability ρ_wrench = (Σⱼ 1/r_j²)^{-1/2}, residual reach and residual base mobility. The selection objective is J = U(ξ) − α C_acq, over 9 surfaces in sticking or sliding mode. Whole-body dynamics: M v̇ + h = Sᵀu + Σ J_g,iᵀ λ_g,i + J_cᵀ λ_c + J_eᵀ w_e. Mean objective was 3.54 with contact selection versus 2.53 with ground contact only (sticking mode). — [Contact as a Decision Variable, arXiv 2609.30140](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30140) **[R]**
- Legs used directly as manipulators: a quadruped opened a spring-damped push door by pressing the handle with a foot, then walked on three legs while pushing the door with that foot. — [Pedipulate, arXiv 2402.10837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10837); see also [Legs as Manipulator, arXiv 2303.11330](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11330) **[R]**

### Inferences
- The student's "crouch onto the lever with a rigid arm" is, in these terms, a series path whose bottleneck is the arm polytope. All the posture-optimisation methods above would only find the arm configuration (via base pose) that best aligns the arm's force-transmission directions with the lever tangent. A ~1.3x gain from re-posing alone is plausible and consistent with the 18-24% SUF gains RoLoMa got from posture optimisation on a strong arm.
- Portela et al.'s 90 N vs 36 N is **not** a clean measure of amplification. Rated payload is a gravity-load spec at a reference pose, not the arm's directional force polytope, so the comparison mixes metrics. A rigorous Go2 + D1 claim should compare polytope directional maxima: arm on a fixed base at the best pose versus whole-body.
- Mahmud et al. 2026 is the closest prior work on the platform and occupies part of the "bracing the body against the environment" niche. It uses a 7-DoF arm and a purpose-built body support frame, and optimises a residual-wrench capability. I saw no evidence that it targets a weak 6-DoF servo arm, a lever-torque task, or near-singular arm load paths.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full Murooka 2015, Nozawa 2012 or Harada 2003 papers for force magnitudes or their exact formulations.
- A search summary attributed to the humanoid-pushing literature the advice that, to push harder against friction, a body should lean forward, lower its CoM and put its feet further back. I could not confirm which paper (possibly arXiv 2303.04985 or the ResearchGate "Pushing and pulling motion generation... whole-body momentum control" paper) says this.
- I found nothing by Carmichael specifically on force-capability posture optimisation within this search budget. The UTS wrench-maximisation line found was Marais, Williams & Pizarro (ACFR, Sydney), not Carmichael.

## 3. Exploiting kinematic singularities or near-singular ("locked-out") configurations to bear large loads with small joint torque

### Takeaway
The principle is textbook. At a singular configuration, Jᵀ has a null space, so wrenches in that null space need zero joint torque: an arm fully extended can in theory resist infinite force along its length. Mechanism designers use it on purpose as "singularity locking" (toggle linkages, four-bar locks), and humanoid knee-stretched walking cuts knee torque by about 77%. Two costs apply. The force capability becomes limited by structure (bearings, links, gearbox), which torque-only polytopes do not model. And compressive loads near singularity are destabilising: the geometric-stiffness term ∂(Jᵀf)/∂q can be negative, so a straight elbow under compression buckles unless it sits on a hard stop or is actively stiffened. I found no robotics paper that deliberately plans near-singular **arm** postures on a mobile or legged robot to exert large task forces such as turning a lever.

### Cited Findings
- "A line of force... [that] passes close to the joints" requires little joint torque. When the arm is fully extended at a singularity one "could theoretically resist infinite forces applied along the arm, because the forces pass through the joints, creating zero torque". — [Lynch & Park, Modern Robotics, Ch. 5](https://modernrobotics.northwestern.edu/nu-gm-book-resource/velocity-kinematics-and-statics/) **[textbook]**
- At a singularity the rank of J (and of Jᵀ) drops below m. The polytope vertex algorithms assume r = m, i.e. "non-singular configurations". — [Skuric et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.05226)
- "Singularity locking" is one of three locking principles (mechanical, friction-based, singularity). "Singularities in mechanisms cause a transfer ratio to go to infinity. In such a singular position, the locking device features an infinitely high locking force and an infinitely small unlocking force." Four-bar linkages are the usual realisation. — [Plooij, Mathijssen, Cherelle, Lefeber & Vanderborght, "Lock your robot: a review of locking devices in robotics", IEEE RAM 22(1), 2015](https://filelist.tudelft.nl/me/Organisatie/Afdelingen/BmechE/DBL/PlooijLocking.pdf)
- Knee-stretched humanoid walking near the knee singularity: the conventional bent-knee posture avoids the singularity "but suffers from large knee joint torque". Extending resolved viscoelasticity control to handle the singularity gave ~77% knee-torque reduction and better energy efficiency. — [Yamamoto, Murotani, Ko & Nakamura, Robotics & Autonomous Systems 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921889022001270); conference version [Murotani et al. 2019](https://researchr.org/publication/MurotaniYKN19) **[R]** (the 77% figure comes from a search-result abstract snippet; I could not open the full text)
- Mechanics of stability under load: δτ = Jᵀδf + (∂Jᵀf₀/∂q)δq + (∂τ_g/∂q)δq. The middle term (load-dependent "geometric stiffness") means torque bounds do not map to linear Cartesian force bounds once the configuration moves under load. — [Ajoudani, Tsagarakis & Bicchi](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/polytopes_stiffness.pdf)
- Mobile manipulators near singularity need special control: Tugal et al. use a forward-dynamics controller "to eliminate undesired excessive motions near singular joint configurations". — [Tugal et al., Autonomous Robots 2022](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/manipulation-at-optimum-locations-for-maximum-force-transmission-/)

### Inferences
- **For the D1 on a lever.** With the elbow straight (or folded to a hard stop) and the force line through the shoulder-pitch and elbow axes, those joints carry little torque. The remaining loads are moments from any offset of the force line, which the wrist and shoulder-yaw servos must hold, plus the arm's gravity torque. In the directional formula of Section 1, a = Jᵀu → small components on the strong-load joints, so s* grows sharply. It is bounded only by the joints whose aᵢ is not zero and, in reality, by unmodelled structural limits.
- **Tension versus compression.** Pulling along a straight arm (tension) is self-stabilising, like a hanging chain. Pushing (compression) is a toggle near its dead point: the geometric-stiffness term is destabilising, and the required joint stiffness grows with load. A "locked-out" push needs either a mechanical stop the load drives into (Plooij's singularity locking) or active stiffness above −∂(Jᵀf)/∂q. If the D1's joint limits are only software limits on servos, the push version may fail by buckling or backdriving.
- **Motion has to come from the body.** At the singularity the arm cannot move the tool along the force line, the velocity–force duality from Modern Robotics. For a lever whose handle moves along the push direction, the body or legs must supply the motion while the straight arm acts as a strut, like a slider-crank with the body as piston, the arm as connecting rod and the lever as crank. As the lever turns, the strut must be kept aligned with the tangent, which is a whole-body planning problem. (This mechanism analogy is my framing; I found no robotics paper that formulates it this way.)
- **Model change needed.** Polytope analysis at or near rank deficiency predicts very large or unbounded forces along the null direction. To report anything meaningful, add structural or servo-gearbox limits and a stability (buckling) check, and report the margin from singularity.

### Gaps
- I found no robotics paper that deliberately uses near-singular arm configurations of a mobile or legged manipulator to exert large forces on the environment, as opposed to load-bearing legs or locking mechanisms. This is not proof that none exists.
- The search snippet "the maximally singular configuration... results in the absolute minimum joint torque for a given tip force" came from a US patent ([US 11,000,945, "Wearable robotic systems for supporting a load"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11000945)). The PDF was image-only, so I could not verify the wording.
- D1 hard-stop locations, structural load ratings and servo backdrive torque are unknown (no source found).

## 4. Bracing: Book, West & Asada and modern revivals

### Takeaway
Bracing means contacting the environment with a point on the arm other than the end effector. It was formulated in the mid-1980s: Book et al. (1984) to make light, flexible arms rigid and precise, and West & Asada (1985) with "mechanical advantage of braced manipulators" and constrained kinematics via virtual closed loops. Delson & West (1993) treated stiffness and natural frequency, and Lee & Kim (1991), Hollis & Hammer (1992), Lew & Book (1994) and Featherstone, Thiebaut & Khatib (1999) extended it. It has been revived for torque-limited collaborative arms (Fang et al. 2019; Johnston et al. 2020; Natarajan et al. 2023; Nakatsuru et al. 2023) and, in Sept 2026, for humanoids (4.4-4.9x usable force range) and for a Go2-based legged manipulator with a body-mounted brace frame. Bracing is the canonical parallel load path that bypasses proximal joints.

### Cited Findings
- **Book (foundational).** Bracing is "a new strategy of robot operation" in which an arm is moved into position and then "rigidized by bracing against either the workpiece or an auxiliary static structure". Subsequent precision motion uses only the degrees of freedom distal to the brace. Light arms need smaller actuators, use less energy, may be faster, and are safer and cheaper. — [Book, Sangveraphunsiri & Le, "The bracing strategy for robot operation", RoManSy 1984](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4615-9882-4_20) **[F]**; publication list: [W.J. Book, Georgia Tech](https://imdl.gatech.edu/wbook/Publications%20070108.htm)
- Bracing micro/macro manipulators: a large flexible arm for gross motion docks with a structure, then a smaller precision workspace operates. This brings control problems from multiple contacts and impact. — [Lew & Book, ICRA 1994 / OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/10163084); [Control of a flexible bracing manipulator, NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930074031) **[F]**
- **West & Asada.** A braced manipulator is constrained by contact at a point other than the end effector. Its kinematics are described by admissible variables, using virtual closed-loop linkages with a single contact and assuming a rigid robot and environment. — summary in [Johnston, Orekhov & Simaan, ICRA 2020 (arXiv 2008.05032)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.05032), citing [West & Asada, ICRA 1985, pp. 251-259](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.05032). A search summary gives a 1985 ASME Winter Annual Meeting paper titled "Kinematic Analysis and Mechanical Advantage of Braced Manipulators"; I could not open it. **[F]**
- Featherstone, Thiebaut & Khatib generalised this to multiple frictionless contacts with screw theory (ICRA 1999). Lee & Kim formulated force and kinematic dexterity ellipsoids (and dynamic manipulability) for systems with bracing (CDC 1991). Delson & West modelled bracing's effect on natural frequency (IJRR 1993). Hollis & Hammer used real and virtual coarse-fine bracing for precision assembly (ICRA 1992). — [reference list of Johnston et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.05032); [Delson & West, IJRR 12(6), 1993](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836499301200604) **[F]**
- **Modern revival, torque-limited arms.**
  - Fang, Kashiri, Rigano, Ajoudani & Tsagarakis make a support contact at the elbow to reduce upper-arm joint effort during wrist-level manipulation, using a three-level hierarchical compliance controller. — [ICRA 2019](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/ICRA.2019.8794119) **[R]**
  - Johnston, Orekhov & Simaan model constrained kinematics and "bracing-consistent compliance" for low-torque collaborative robots reaching into confined spaces, and use redundancy to minimise directional compliance under the brace. — [ICRA 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.05032) **[R]**
  - Natarajan, Johnston, Simaan, Likhachev & Choset plan torque-limited whole-arm manipulation through contact. A Kinova Gen3 experiment shows bracing contacts reduce the total torque needed compared with the same trajectory in free space. — [ICRA 2023](http://biorobotics.ri.cmu.edu/papers/paperUploads/85259-2703.pdf) **[R]**
  - Nakatsuru, Wan & Harada plan contact-rich motion so that a mobile manipulator with a collaborative arm moves objects beyond its maximum payload, using environment support. — [arXiv 2302.13212](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.13212) **[R]**
  - A 2026 Scientific Reports paper induces environmental support contact through redundancy resolution, because "interaction forces acting at a distance amplify joint torques through the lever-arm effect" in extended-tool tasks. — [Enabling extended tool manipulation through redundancy-driven contact exploitation, Sci. Rep. 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-60582-y) (abstract snippet only; full text not accessible) **[R]**
- **Humanoid bracing (Sept 2026).** One hand braces on the environment while the other applies force. Quasi-static wrench balance F = f_g + f_w + f_s with a ZMP constraint, friction penalties and the quartic torque-utilisation penalty. Real-robot usable force range went from 13.5 N (no bracing) to 60 N at centre and upper positions (40 N lower), "~4.4-4.9x". Normal-force RMS error was roughly halved and shoulder wobble fell 2.7-3.8x. Related work cited includes Book (1985), Fang et al. (2019) and Wang & Minami (2010) on multi-contact mobile manipulators. — [Zhang, Lehnert, Browne & Roberts, "Brace Yourself", arXiv 2609.25486](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25486v1) **[R]**
- **Legged-manipulator bracing (Sept 2026):** Go2 + NERO arm + body-mounted support frame; contact selection by residual wrench capability. — [Mahmud, Li & Wang, arXiv 2609.30140](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30140) **[R]**

### Inferences
- **Load-path algebra for bracing (derived from the floating-base equation in Mahmud et al.).** With a brace contact force λ_b at body or arm point b: τ = g(q) − J_eᵀ w_e − J_bᵀ λ_b, with λ_b in its friction cone. The directional maximum becomes an LP: maximise s subject to τ_min ≤ g − J_eᵀ(s·u) − J_bᵀλ_b ≤ τ_max and λ_b ∈ FC_b. For joints proximal to b, the J_bᵀλ_b term can cancel the J_eᵀw_e term. For joints distal to b it cannot, so the bottleneck moves to the distal joints, which for the D1 are the wrist servos.
- **Three Go2 + D1 bracing variants, in descending order of arm-joint bypass:**
  1. The Go2 body (or a passive bumper or frame on it) presses the lever or door directly, so the arm only positions or latches.
  2. The D1 forearm or wrist is braced against the box or door frame near the handle, so shoulder and elbow are bypassed and only the wrist carries the lever moment.
  3. The Go2 body braces against the box so the feet are not friction-limited. This helps balance and friction but does not help the arm, because it is still in series.
- West & Asada's "mechanical advantage of braced manipulators" is the classical name for exactly the student's "force paths that bypass the arm motors". Citing it frames the work as a legged and quantitative revival rather than a new concept.

### Gaps
- I could not read West & Asada's 1985 papers or Book 1984 in full, so their exact mechanical-advantage definitions are not reproduced.
- I could not find D1-scale numbers (how much bracing helps a low-torque servo arm on a lever).

## 5. Mechanical advantage via tools and levers in robot tool use

### Takeaway
Tool-use planning with explicit torque and friction limits exists. Holladay, Lozano-Pérez & Rodriguez's "forceful kinematic chain" constraint propagates the task wrench through robot, grasp, tool and environment, and chooses grasps and tools so that no joint torque or grasp friction limit is exceeded; it prefers grasps that shorten adverse moment arms. The lever principle is elementary: mechanical advantage = effort arm / load arm. Moment arms only help when the reaction goes into a fulcrum or the environment, not when the tool is held cantilevered by the weak wrist.

### Cited Findings
- Holladay et al. add to task-and-motion planning a "forceful kinematic chain constraint" that "explicitly consider[s] torque and frictional limits", choosing grasps, arm paths and tool paths subject to joint torque limits, grasp stability and collisions. Domains: opening a child-proof bottle, twisting a nut, cutting a vegetable. Robust planning picked knife grasps closer to the blade because that creates "a smaller torque that the grasp needs to resist". — [Holladay, Lozano-Pérez & Rodriguez, "Robust planning for multi-stage forceful manipulation", IJRR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00319); [IJRR DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/02783649231198560); precursor [Force-and-Motion Constrained Planning for Tool Use (IROS 2019)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356148868_Force-and-Motion_Constrained_Planning_for_Tool_Use) and [Holladay MIT thesis](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/639d6466-943c-40b9-89e6-ba619c946234/content) **[R]**
- With extended tools on torque-limited arms, "interaction forces acting at a distance amplify joint torques through the lever-arm effect". The cited remedy is to induce environmental support contact. — [Sci. Rep. 2026 abstract](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-60582-y) **[R]**
- Lever mechanical advantage is the ratio of effort arm to load arm. — [Firgelli, Lever mechanism explainer](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/lever) (low-authority general reference; standard physics)
- On a legged robot, RoLoMa's tasks include pulling a lever and turning a hand wheel, analysed with the SUF. — [RoLoMa](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446)

### Inferences
- **For a lever handle:** the required tangential force is F_t = M_req / r_grip. Grasping or pushing at the handle's tip rather than near the hinge is the cheapest gain, linear in r_grip, and needs no hardware change.
- **A held extension rod does not help on its own.** A rod clamped in the D1 gripper to lengthen r_grip lowers F_t, but the gripper and wrist must then react the rod's moment. The wrist sees about M_req again, so the bottleneck moves to the wrist servos. A tool gives true mechanical advantage only when it pivots on a **fulcrum** on the environment or the robot body (class-1/2 lever), or when it is mounted on the Go2 body (a parallel path), so that the reaction bypasses the arm.
- Holladay's forceful-kinematic-chain constraint is the natural planning formalism to extend: add the legged body as a chain element, and allow body-mounted tools or fulcrum contacts.

### Gaps
- I found no robot tool-use paper that specifically plans **leverage via a fulcrum** (crowbar or pry-bar style) with joint-torque-limit reasoning for a legged platform. Within the search budget I also found no paper that verified the reported nut-twisting tool used a wrench for leverage.

## 6. Human biomechanics: how humans exert force beyond arm strength (brief)

### Takeaway
Human push and pull strength depends strongly on posture, handle height, foot-floor friction and, above all, on whether the body can brace against a support. Kroemer's 1960s-70s studies and recent work show that bracing adds a parallel force path through the body. Humans also pick arm configurations rather than co-contraction to resist loads, and joint-torque-based force feasible sets (polytopes) predict human hand-force capacity better than ellipsoids.

### Cited Findings
- Kroemer measured horizontal push and pull forces in common standing working positions on surfaces of different traction. He distinguished reaction forces supplied only by shoe-floor friction from those supplied by vertical bracing surfaces that the subject anchors feet or body against. — [Kroemer, "Horizontal push and pull forces exertable when standing in working positions on various surfaces", Applied Ergonomics 1974](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0003687074900854); [AMRL-TR-68-143, Push forces exerted in sixty-five common working positions (1968)](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD0695040.pdf) **[F]**
- Summary of Kroemer (1974) in a 2026 study: the maximal one-hand force "is increased significantly when the body can exert pressure against an external support, due to added chain of force vectors through the body". The bracing surface mitigates both postural and foot-friction constraints. — [Influence of floor inclination and bracing surface availability on hand force production capacity... (Int. J. Industrial Ergonomics, 2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169814126000892) **[R]**
- Chaffin, Andres & Garg studied the postures people choose for maximal push and pull exertions in the sagittal plane. — [Human Factors 25(5):541-550, 1983](https://doi.org/10.1177/001872088302500508) **[F]**. Two-handed pull-push strength depended on gender, pull or push, isometric or isokinetic mode, plane and handle height; all subjects were strongest pulling isometrically in the sagittal plane at medium height. — [Kumar, Narayan & Bacchus, Human Factors 1995](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872095778995526) **[F]**
- Humans choose arm configurations to obtain a desired endpoint impedance. — [Ajoudani et al.](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/polytopes_stiffness.pdf)
- Joint-torque-based polytopes and ellipsoids predict human upper-limb force feasible sets with posture-dependent accuracy. Over the force-ellipsoid surface, only 39.7% of points were within 50 N of measured values. — [Rezzoug, Hernandez & Gorce 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomechanics1010008); related [Simulation Study of the Upper-limb Wrench Feasible Set with Glenohumeral Joint Constraints (Skuric et al.)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.07487) **[R]**

### Inferences
- The human evidence supports the robot formulation. Body weight helps only to the extent that a parallel path (bracing, feet against a stop) or a better-aligned limb (posture) is available. A person leaning on a weak, bent arm is still limited by that arm, which is the human counterpart of the student's series-path result.

### Gaps
- I did not find, within budget, a human door-opening or lever-turning study that quantifies body-lean strategies. Search results were dominated by generic push/pull ergonomics.

## 7. Applying this to a legged robot whose arm is the torque bottleneck, and is it novel?

### Takeaway
A rigorous framing for the Go2 + D1 is this. (1) Compute the arm's **directional force polytope** (exact, gravity-biased), which gives the lever force and torque bound and the binding joint for any arm configuration. (2) Treat the body as changing the arm's base pose and adding contacts, so that the legs' AWP/FWP (Orsolino) enters only through **intersection** for series paths. (3) Model bypasses as **Minkowski-sum or LP** extensions: brace contacts (West & Asada; Book), body or leg contact on the lever (pedipulation), or fulcrum tools. (4) Treat near-singular "strut" postures as a separate regime that needs structural and stability limits. The individual ingredients are all published. The September 2026 papers (humanoid bracing, Go2 contact selection) show the idea is active and near-contemporaneous. The student's explicit load-path decomposition for a torque-bottlenecked servo arm on a legged base, applied to lever torque, may still be a defensible contribution, but the novelty claim must be narrow and must cite these works.

### Cited Findings
- Legged feasibility sets are built per limb from joint torque limits and combined by Minkowski sum, then intersected with contact cones. — [Orsolino et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.02731)
- Polytope sum and intersection operators, with millisecond vertex enumeration, are available off the shelf. — [pycapacity docs](https://auctus-team.gitlabpages.inria.fr/people/antunskuric/pycapacity/pycapacity.robot.html); [Skuric et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.05226)
- Worst-case and task-directed residual-capability metrics for floating-base robots: SUF ([RoLoMa](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01446)), signed distance to the residual force polytope ([FORTE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21497)), and residual wrench capability for contact selection on a Go2 ([Mahmud et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30140)).
- Demonstrated whole-body force beyond the arm's rating: 90 N vs 36 N rated payload on B1 + Z1 ([Portela et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01402v1)). Bracing on a humanoid: 13.5 N to 60 N ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25486v1)).

### Inferences
- **Suggested analysis pipeline (quasi-static) for each candidate stance:**
  1. Solve the arm IK for the handle contact.
  2. Compute s* = directional polytope maximum along the lever tangent with gravity bias, and record the binding joint.
  3. Lever torque = s*·r_grip.
  4. Check the body side: is the CoM inside the actuation-aware feasible region, are the foot forces inside their friction cones and leg force polytopes, and is the body pose kinematically feasible?
  5. Repeat with each bypass added (brace contact λ_b, body contact, foot contact) as an LP.
  6. Near-singular postures: also report the margin from singularity (σ_min of the arm J) and a buckling check from ∂(Jᵀf)/∂q.
- **Report gains as ratios of directional polytope maxima** (whole-body / fixed-base best pose), not against rated payload. This also explains the existing ~1.3x as "posture-only, series path".
- **Novelty, judged against what I found:**
  - Not novel: force polytopes, posture optimisation for force, legged FWP, bracing (1984-85), environmental-contact effort reduction (2019-2023), whole-body force beyond arm rating (2024), humanoid bracing (2026), Go2 contact selection with a body brace frame (2026).
  - Possibly novel, not found in my search: (a) an explicit series-versus-parallel load-path decomposition (intersection vs sum) as the explanation for why legged body weight does not amplify a torque-bottlenecked arm; (b) deliberate near-singular "strut" arm postures with the legged body supplying motion, for lever or door actuation; (c) the quantitative case of a low-cost servo arm (D1-class) where the arm, not the base, is the limit.
  - Not finding (a)-(c) is not proof of absence. Mahmud et al. 2026 and Zhang et al. 2026 should be read in full before claiming them.

### Gaps
- I did not search the newest legged loco-manipulation work exhaustively (e.g., Spot or ANYmal door opening with body assistance, Sleiman et al. multicontact planning in Science Robotics 2023). Another researcher may cover these.
- No published D1 force or torque characterisation was found to anchor the polytope numbers. The published joint torque limits used by the student were not re-verified here.
